## Basic Instruction
> \*Always leave 1 rep in the tank, never work up to failure but get as close as you can.
>
> \*\*Do steady cardio whenever you want, just don't overdo it... I recommend doing it on your aerobic days, max 40 min of steady.
>
> \*\*\*The lower the reps, the higher the weight... Always go for 1-3 RIR (reps in reserve), aka RPE 7+.
>
> \*\*\*\*When there are joint pains, usually going unilateral and/or getting away from fixed grips (barbells, bars) and going to split grips (dumbbells, kettlebells, individual grips) helps take care of some stuff.
>
> \*\*\*\*\*If you have any questions just ask me - IG @BLAZECAMP or TWITTER @BLAZECAMP\_

Planned effort across the stairway: weeks 1-6: leave 3 in the tank; weeks 7-9: leave 2 in the tank; weeks 10-11: leave 1-2 in the tank; week 12: leave 1 in the tank. The last week may add one intensifier on a small-muscle exercise.

How heavy? Each exercise tells you how many reps to leave in the tank: "leave 2 in the tank" means you could do 2 more reps at the end of the set (about RPE 8; 3 left = RPE 7, 1 left = RPE 9). The first weeks leave more in the tank, the last weeks less. Week 1: start a little lighter, with a weight you could lift 2-3 more times.

Olympic lifts are for power. Cleans, snatches and their dumbbell and kettlebell versions train power: move the weight as fast as you can with clean technique. Pick a weight you can move explosively on every rep; if it slows down or your technique breaks, it's too heavy. You don't need percentages for these unless you compete in Olympic weightlifting.

Your plan climbs like a stairway: small steps first, a bit bigger after the halfway week, the hardest weeks at the end.
