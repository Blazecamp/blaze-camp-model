## Basic Instruction
> \*Instead of working to failure, push your limits over the course of time.
>
> \*\*CrossFit has its hybrid style of performance, so to be competitive, you MUST be able to be as good as possible in EACH modality. Train accordingly.
>
> \*\*\*The WODs (workouts of the day) in this plan are your conditioning on these days. Do them instead of your gym's WOD, not on top of it.
>
> \*\*\*\*\*If you have any questions just ask me - IG @BLAZECAMP or TWITTER @BLAZECAMP\_

How hard each week is: weeks 1-4: leave 3 in the tank; weeks 5-6: leave 2 in the tank; week 7: leave 1-2 in the tank; week 8: leave 1 in the tank. Week 8: one extra-hard set on Friday. Use the day's last small-muscle or machine exercise. Do a MYO set (see the note on the exercise).

How heavy? Each exercise says how many reps to leave in the tank. "Leave 2 in the tank" means you could still do 2 more reps. That is about an 8 out of 10 for effort. (3 left is a 7. 1 left is a 9.) Your plan starts with 3 left. Then it goes to 2, then 1-2. In the last week it is 1, with a few extra-hard sets. Week 1: start a little lighter. Pick a weight you could lift about 3 more times.

Olympic lifts are for power. Cleans, snatches and their dumbbell and kettlebell versions train power. Move the weight as fast as you can, with good form. Pick a weight you can move fast on every rep. If it slows down or your form slips, it is too heavy. You do not need percentages for these. You only need them if you compete in weightlifting.

Core supersets are optional. You can pair a core exercise with any exercise to save time.

## How you progress

- Reps and weight follow a rule you apply yourself, not a number this plan promises. Every lift shows a range of reps. Example from your plan: Standing DB Calf Raise (deficit optional), 3 sets of 10-12. Say you hit 12 on every set, with good form and the planned reps left in the tank. Then add weight next time and start again at 10. Not there yet? Keep the weight and add a rep where you can.
- The plan adds sets week by week, like steps. Each muscle group reaches its final sets in the last week. The week-by-week table shows this. Some weeks keep the same sets as the week before. In those weeks, add reps or weight.
- Your next block starts a little higher than this one did: about 5 % more sets, in whole sets. It never goes past the top number for your level. At the top, the sets stay. Then you add weight and reps.
- You can edit this plan. Change the sets of any exercise on any day or week. Add or take out exercises. Turn blocks off.

## How to adjust this plan

- Missed most of a week? Do that week again before you move on. Missed one day? Do it on the next free day, or let it go. Never squeeze two days into one.
- A set felt like your max, with no rep left? Next time, take one set off that exercise, or a little weight.
- Everything feels easy? First add a little weight or a rep. Never skip ahead more than one week.
- Something hurts? First use a listed swap, then cut that exercise's sets by a third to a half, then drop it for the rest of the block. See a professional if it keeps hurting.
- Life got in the way for a few weeks? Maybe you were sick, away or busy. Stop the block. Take the easy week if you want. Then build a new block.
- You or your coach can turn off these blocks on any day: power / Olympic lifts, core, skill, conditioning, cardio and accessories. You can also add or take out exercises, like more rear shoulder work. Use the suggested supersets to save time.
- Switching off the accessories block takes away every set just for Biceps, Triceps and Calves.
- You or your coach can also change the sets of any exercise, on any day or week. The plan is a starting point you can edit.
- The core block is optional. You may turn it off or change its exercises on any day. You can change anything in this plan.
- Each day shows an average time. It is an estimate, just so you know. Nothing in this plan is cut or changed because of time. Want a shorter day? Use the suggested supersets. Or take out what the day lists as easiest to take out.
