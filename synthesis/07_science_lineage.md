# 07 · Science lineage map: where every Blaze Camp idea comes from

**What this is.** A map from every concept found in the coach's two workbooks ('BUILD YOUR OWN PROGRAM - BLAZE CAMP MODEL' = **BYOP**, 'BLAZE CAMP MASTER MODEL' = **MM**) to its most likely origin. For each concept it shows where the coach applies the source faithfully, where he adapts it, and where the idea is his own. It also places the packaged models he says he does *not* build on (5/3/1, Texas Method, Prilepin's chart, Westside conjugate) and shows where his sheets echo them anyway. It ends with the first-principles rules the generator has to encode (v1: 4-6-week maintenance / transition, 8-week and 12-week plans, plus open-ended week-by-week and yearly modes, COACH-ANSWER PER-01).

**Audience.** The coach, to check that this is a fair account of his thinking and where it comes from. The engineer, to know which rules rest on solid evidence, which are the coach's own design choices (and so must stay configurable), and which conflict with the evidence and need his decision.

**Evidence tags (used on every claim).**

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **OBSERVED** | Read from a cell, colour or formula in the coach's sheets (tab + cell or area cited; labels quoted verbatim, typos included: 'SAGITAL', 'TRIPPLE', 'INTERMIDIATE', 'CLERANCE'). |
| **INFERRED** | My reasoning. A proposal the coach can overrule. |
| **SCIENCE** | External source: peer-reviewed literature, or the named practitioner's own published material. Reference IDs like [D1], [I3], [S24] resolve in section 18 with URLs. Each source there is marked *primary*, *secondary* (a reliable summary) or *search-summary only* (the page blocked direct reading; the claim comes from a search-engine summary and is weaker). |

**Lineage verdicts (one per concept).**

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FAITHFUL** | The coach applies the source idea essentially as the source teaches it. |
| **ADAPTED** | The idea comes from a source, but the coach changed its parameters, structure or scope. |

| **GAP** | The source has it; the coach's system lacks it. |

**What changed**
1. **CAT-04, CAT-10 - Davidson zones.** The coach names Pat Davidson's book *A Coach's Guide to Optimizing Movement* as the source of his "zones". In his words the zones are rotation biases: *vertical* positions - hands down / standing (zone 1), hands overhead or a deep squat (zone 3; the deep squat's zone number is INFERRED) - carry an **external-rotation bias**; *horizontal* positions - hands in front of you, or the knee at hip height halfway through a squat (zone 2) - carry an **internal-rotation bias and are more compressive**. As levels: zone 1 foundational, zone 2 intermediate ("high-pressure ... but it's safer for you when you push and pull"), zone 3 advanced; machines and cables make any zone easier; zones do not apply to plyos. New row in 1.1; B16 and C1 updated; rule 5 extended. A web search (2026-10-08) found no public description of this zone wording, so the lineage reads "attributed by the coach to Davidson; consistent with the PRI / Hartman / Davidson pressure model (expansion ~ external rotation, compression ~ internal rotation) [D10][P1]" rather than verified.
2. **CAT-05.** Levels are still theoretical: zones and tiers give a default order and suggestions, never a gate (C1).
3. **CAT-13 - contralateral / ipsilateral.** A contralateral load *pulls you into the hip* (the eccentric coils you into the stance hip, then you create force away from it); an ipsilateral load makes getting in harder and *helps pull you out of the hip*. Use cases: change-of-direction athletes (football route runners, soccer, basketball, tennis) to get in and out of the hip quickly and build the ranges they lack; ipsilateral step-ups for sprint acceleration; a health accessory for bodybuilders and powerlifters, mostly in the first 2-4 weeks before the program turns bilateral-symmetrical dominant, kept later only if something bothers them. The v0 joint reading (contralateral = internal rotation / adduction of the stance hip) is consistent with this; the v0 "contralateral is the default" assumption is retired. 1.2, B2 and rule 7 rewritten. Where he learned it was not answered.
4. **CAT-10, CAT-11 - plyo "zones".** Not zones: "the way I want the plyos to be done" - receive and pause → receive then create → repeated receive / create, with more receiving early in a program; unilateral harder than bilateral. He has not named it (placeholder: plyo execution method). C8 and rule 10 rewritten.
5. **ATH-01, CAT-10 - ballistic vs plyometric.** The cut is ground-contact time: "The longer they're on the ground, the less it is going to be plyo, so it's more of a ballistic movement." The sport's own ground-contact time sets the ratio; programs start ballistic and become more plyometric. D1, rule 9 and section 12 updated.
6. **VOL-09.** Helper credit is 0.25, not 0.5 (rule 2 field list).
7. Section 17: questions 1, 3, 5 and 19 marked answered or partly answered; two new questions (22, 23) added.
8. **Retired ideas marked in place outside the zones scope (verification passes).** Variable resistance DORMANT (EQ-01, EQ-12: B10, D5, D7, H11, rules 3, 10, 12, 23, 31, contribution 2); machines not ranked (EQ-11: B15, rule 26); plan shapes (PER-01: H4, rule 21); core first by default (ATH-11: F1, rule 8); size-based 3/5/7 % and 0.5 synergists retired (VOL-05, VOL-09: H1, T4, rules 16, 20); exercise variation (PER-02, PER-03, PER-04: section 0, F11, T3, rule 27, Q11); contrast rules (ATH-12: D7, T7, rule 12, Q7); aerobic sessions per week (ATH-07: rule 25); knee + pull / hip + push reason (UNI-11, ORD-06: F3, rule 30, Q16); Thursday = strength (PRG-09: D16); RIR primary at 1-3 (VOL-13: T2, Q10).

1. **The library architecture is Pat Davidson's Rethinking the Big Patterns (RTBP) taxonomy, almost cell for cell.** RTBP has 13 motor patterns grouped under three families, Control, Athletic and Resistance [D2][D3][D4][D8]. The BYOP 'MASTER EXERCISE LIBRARY' row 4 'PATTERN' uses exactly 'CONTROL', 'ATHLETIC' and 'RESISTANCE' (OBSERVED O4:T4, C4:N4, U4:AU4). The coach's zone codes in Sheet23 row 1 abbreviate two of those families ('AP' = athletic patterns, 'RP' = resistance patterns); the CONTROL family is not coded as a family but by its two regions, 'CT' (core thorax, Sheet23 N1:P1 = BYOP O-Q) and 'CP' (core pelvis, Sheet23 Q1:S1 = BYOP R-T) (OBSERVED by column match; expansion of the letters INFERRED). This is the strongest lineage in the whole system.
2. **The dose layer is Mike Israetel / Renaissance Periodization.** The coach's stated rules ("15-20 sets per muscle", "more for the muscle you want to grow, fewer with pain", "everybody picks between 5 and 30 reps", "smaller muscles take more reps because you can't really go that heavy") are RP's volume landmarks and rep guidance almost word for word [I1][I3][I6]. ### 1.2 The PRI / Bill Hartman / Diane Lee school (Davidson's own influences)

* Postural Restoration Institute (PRI) language is about getting **into** a hip: "Left AF IR" = left acetabulum-on-femur internal rotation, reached by shifting onto that side, reaching with the opposite arm, and using exhale-driven trunk positioning [P1].
* The split-squat school derived from it treats the hand holding the load as a reach. A contralateral load rotates the pelvis **toward** the front leg (into internal rotation and adduction). An ipsilateral load rotates it **away** [P2][P3].
* EMG evidence (Stastny et al. 2015): contralateral loading raised gluteus medius activity in the dynamic forward lunge but not in the static split squat [P4].
* v1, the coach's own explanation (COACH-ANSWER CAT-13) matches this school: a contralateral load *pulls you into the hip* (coil into the stance hip on the way down, then create force away from it); an ipsilateral load makes getting in harder and *helps pull you out*. He uses both for change-of-direction athletes (football route runners, soccer, basketball, tennis), ipsilateral step-ups for sprint acceleration ("internal rotation on the leg, but external rotation on the hip"), and both as a health accessory for bodybuilders and powerlifters, mostly in the first 2-4 weeks. He did not say where he learned it.
* Bill Hartman and Diane Lee (thorax-pelvis relationship) are named in the show notes of a Davidson interview [D9]. Davidson's own pressure model (expansion vs compression) is the same school [D10].

### 1.4 First-principles science used in this map

| Principle | Core claim (SCIENCE) | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Specificity (SAID) | the body adapts specifically to the demands imposed on it | [S1] |

## 12. The sport dial: basketball > MMA > boxing/karate (lineage of the coach's central claim)

**The claim (coach, paraphrased).** Basketball players need more plyos, boxers and karate athletes fewer, MMA in between; some athletes need more resistance work, others more power and explosiveness with athletic patterns.

**Origin.** The principle is **specificity (SAID)** [S1] applied through **Davidson's athletic-pattern taxonomy**. A sport's demands (jumping, cutting, striking, rotating) decide which athletic patterns get volume [D2]. Davidson's ballistic vs plyometric split [D1], which the coach writes as 'BALLISTIC PURPOSE' vs 'PLYOMETRIC PURPOSE', supplies the mechanism: plyometric work trains "REACTIVE CONNECTIVE TISSUE ADAPTATIONS" (fast SSC, stiffness, tendon load); ballistic work trains "MUSCULAR FORCE PRODUCTION".

**v1 (COACH-ANSWER ATH-01, ATH-02).** The coach confirmed the ranking and named the mechanism: ground-contact time. More time on the ground (karate, fighters) → more ballistics, fewer plyos; less time on the ground (sprinters) → more plyos; basketball a mix; baseball ballistics, rotational throws and sprinting with few plyos; both ends always stay in the program. That is the fast / slow SSC distinction [S4] applied to the sport, so the mechanism is FAITHFUL to SSC science; the per-sport numbers live in `04_athlete_profiles.md`.

## 16. Principles the generator must encode (numbered first-principles rules)

Each rule gives the **rule**, the **encoding**, its **lineage** (reference IDs) and the **coach knob** (what he can change without code). All rules are INFERRED syntheses of OBSERVED sheet logic and SCIENCE.

1. **Specificity first.** Every program is built from the client's goal and sport demands. The goal picks a slot skeleton (AESTHETICS, ATHLETIC, PPL, BOOTCAMP, STRENGTH, HYBRID, FAT LOSS); the sport sets the athletic dials. *Lineage:* SAID [S1], RP specificity [I4], Davidson [D2]. *Knob:* goal → template map.
2. **Classify before you select.** Every exercise record carries `family` (ATHLETIC / CONTROL / RESISTANCE / CARDIO / FUNCTIONAL / ISOLATION), `pattern` (Davidson's 13 + isolation set), `stance` (bilateral symmetrical / staggered / split / single-leg / lateral), `plane` (dominant external moment), `level`, `load_position`, `load_laterality` (bilateral / contralateral / ipsilateral, with a use case - COACH-ANSWER CAT-13), `davidson_zone` (zone 1 / 2 / 3 with its rotation bias - CAT-04, CAT-10), `implement`, `velocity_intent`, `primary_muscles` (1.0) and helpers (0.25, COACH-ANSWER VOL-09; was 0.5). *Lineage:* RTBP quality/quantity [D6][D7]; RP muscle accounting [I1].
3. **An exercise is a base plus variables.** Choose a base within the client's tier cap, then add variables (stance, load position, into/out of hip, ROM, end range, feet position, grip; accommodating resistance is DORMANT - no bands, chains or weight releasers for now, COACH-ANSWER EQ-01, EQ-12) while the summed difficulty stays within the tier budget. v1 timing (COACH-ANSWER PER-02, PER-04): keep the exercises fixed for the 4-6 week block, change one or two variables (grip, stance, implement, ROM, rep range) at the next block; the second weekly exposure of a slot takes a different exercise from the same category. *Lineage:* Davidson's progression principles [D6]; coach's RESISTANCE PATTERNS grammar. *Knob:* the variable tables and their difficulty weights.
4. **Planes progress sagittal → frontal → transverse.** Default sequencing for core and athletic work, and the regression path under fatigue or pain ("sagittal city"). The coach's own filing uses plane = tier for core (toes-to-bar FOUNDATIONAL, Russian twists ADVANCED); treating plane as a sequence with per-exercise overrides is an analyst proposal pending Q4. *Lineage:* Jacksonian dissolution / evolutionary ladder [D1][D13].
5. **Stability progression.** Supported → seated → tall kneeling → half kneeling → split → standing → single-leg; DB before barbell within a base; feet on bench/wall → floor → hollow. v1: the arm position adds the coach's Davidson-zone order - zone 1 (arms down) → zone 2 (horizontal, in front) → zone 3 (overhead); load in front → on the body → behind; supported, machine and cable versions lower the demand (COACH-ANSWER CAT-04). A default order only: levels are theoretical (CAT-05). *Lineage:* Davidson [D6]; Cook/Voight developmental postures [S40]; zones as the coach attributes them to *A Coach's Guide to Optimizing Movement*.
6. **Load position steers the knee-dominant stimulus.** Centred/low → goblet/front → zercher → front rack/SSB → high bar → low bar → overhead. Front-loaded for a quad/upright goal; back-loaded for powerlifting style ("heavier, fewer reps"). Posture difficulty is not program level. *Lineage:* biomechanics [S24]; coach's ALL ABOUT SQUATS.
7. **Into / out of the hip.** In unilateral work a contralateral load pulls the athlete *into* the stance hip (coil in on the way down, then create force away); an ipsilateral load makes getting in harder and helps *drive out* of the hip (COACH-ANSWER CAT-13). Joint reading: contralateral = internal rotation / adduction demand, glute-med emphasis in dynamic lunges; ipsilateral = anti-abduction, trunk demand [P2][P3][P4]. Use: change-of-direction athletes (football route runners, soccer, basketball, tennis) to get in and out of the hip quickly and build range; ipsilateral step-ups for sprint acceleration; a health accessory for bodybuilders and powerlifters, mostly in the first 2-4 weeks ("the first couple of weeks, 3 weeks, up to 4"), then mostly bilateral, kept later only if something bothers them. No global default. Suitcase < front-racked. Split-stance single-arm pulls are always contralateral. Program every asymmetrical drill in R/L pairs. *Lineage:* PRI/Hartman/Davidson [P1][P2][D14]; EMG [P4]; coach CAT-13.
8. **Fast before slow.** Session order: positional warm-up → fast SSC / plyo → throws → COD → triple extension / heavy ballistics → bilateral strength → unilateral → isolation → conditioning buy-out. v1 (COACH-ANSWER ATH-11, NOT RIGHT on the v0 'core after the power block' reading): the core block comes FIRST by default, about 2 exercises that double as the warm-up, and the user may move it after the plyos or after strength; the v0 placement of core at the end or as Part B is retired. Never fatigue the athlete before plyometric or sprint work. *Lineage:* Davidson's session structure [D1]; NSCA [S7].
9. **Ballistic ≠ plyometric.** Two dials: ballistic (force production, longer contact, max intent: Olympic lifts, loaded jumps, medball throws) and plyometric (reactive, short contact, stiffness: rebounding, pogo, depth drops). Sport mostly changes rebounding amplitude and volume; striking and rotational sports keep ballistic and throw volume high. v1: the cut is ground-contact time (COACH-ANSWER ATH-01) - longer contact = ballistic, shorter = plyometric; the sport's ground-contact time sets the ratio, and programs start ballistic and become more plyometric (CAT-10). *Lineage:* Davidson [D1]; fast/slow SSC [S4]; Verkhoshansky [S6]; combat-sport plyo meta [S55].
10. **Plyometric progression.** Plyo execution method (not a zone; name pending - COACH-ANSWER CAT-10, CAT-11): receive and pause → receive then create → repeated receive / create, with more receiving early in a program; force phases 'TAKING IN FORCES' → 'DELIVER FORCES FAST' → 'AMORTIZATION OF FORCES' → 'REBOUNDING'; bilateral → unilateral; unloaded → weighted (drop-release last); no drop → drop; hop (ankle) → jump (full); reverse-banded variants dormant while variable resistance is out (EQ-01, EQ-12). Depth jumps only after landing competence and a strength base. *Lineage:* NSCA [S7]; Verkhoshansky [S6]; AEL [S26]; coach's BALLISTICS tab.
11. **Plyos are dosed in contacts.** Track high-amplitude and low-amplitude contacts separately (the split is an analyst convention; the coach files squirrel jumps as ADVANCED 'FULL JUMPING'); per-session ceilings follow NSCA bands (80-100 / 100-120 / 120-140) by default, as a coach knob, since his own programs concentrate up to 136-146 contacts into one session (MM STARTING S1, MID M1); 48-72 h between high-intensity plyo days; never more than a 20 % weekly jump except at a phase change. Athletic exposure is not hypertrophy volume but is charged to the recovery budget. *Lineage:* [S7][S54][S55][S56]. *Knob:* sport tier table.

27. **Vary exercises the coach's way, but keep progress trackable.** v1 (COACH-ANSWER PER-02, PER-03, PER-04): the exercises stay fixed for the 4-6-week block and the next block changes one or two variables (grip, stance, implement, ROM, rep range); within a week, the second exposure of a slot takes a different exercise from the same category, and for squats that second exposure avoids axial loading (leg press or belt squat, since squats and deadlifts already load the spine); A/B lead-lift weeks are optional, for clients who want variety. The v0 example 'front-loaded early week, back-loaded later' (Front Squat Mon → Low Bar Back Squat Thu) is retired because it loads the spine twice. *Lineage:* Davidson [D1]; [S46]; [I7]. *Knob:* `variation_mode` (default fixed within block; A/B weeks opt-in).
28. **Pain and feedback rules.** 'Felt a PAIN' → swap fixed grip to split grip, or bilateral to unilateral, in the same pattern; if it repeats → −1 set; then drop a tier. 'Felt the WRONG muscle' → change load position or stance in the same slot. "Couldn't do it" → drop one tier. EFFORT 9-10 → hold progression; ≤6 → progress load or reps. *Lineage:* coach's A6/A7 and logbook; RP autoregulation [I2].
29. **Levels mean different things for different people.** Gym clients: a higher level means more weekly sets (UL 76 → 90 → 102); compound reps mostly fall (UL lower day 12 → 10 → 8, PPL push/pull 15 → 10 → 8) but UL upper-day compounds rise 8 → 10 → 12, so rep direction per block is a coach setting; arm isolation stays fixed at 20 / 'MAX REPS' while PPL delt isolation gets heavier (3 × 15 → 4 × 8). Athletes: a higher level means fewer exercises and sets, higher intensity, more unilateral and reactive work, and more throws. The exercise tier is chosen per slot, not per person. *Lineage:* [I4][S13]; coach's MM levels.
30. **Knee + pull / hip + push (or bilateral/unilateral crossing) inside a session; main patterns twice a week.** Pairing is the coach's own rule (no external source), now explained: back-of-the-legs work pairs with front-of-the-upper-body work and knee-dominant with pulling, so fatigue does not bleed over between neighbouring muscles; the same rule holds for full-body and bootcamp days (COACH-ANSWER UNI-11, ORD-06). Twice a week is evidence-supported; the second squat exposure is non-axial (PER-04). The coach keeps 2× in his 4- and 6-day templates but lets vertical push/pull and the single-leg hinge drop to 1× in '3 DAY BUILDER', and BASEBALL alternates horizontal and vertical upper patterns (1× each) (F4). *Lineage:* coach (OWN); [S17][I1].
31. **Accommodating resistance - DORMANT in v1.** The generator prescribes no bands, chains or weight releasers for now (COACH-ANSWER EQ-01: 'I don't do bands'; EQ-12: 'No variable resistance should be available in this for now. We will work with that later on'), and offers no band substitutes (EQ-07, EQ-08). Archive of the v0 rule, for when he reopens it: advanced barbell work only, chosen by diagnosis - can't yield → bottom bands (~50 %, speed); slow on the way up → reverse bands (lower load, fast); needs to stay stiff through eccentric, amortization and concentric → chains (~70 %, stiffness); weight releasers disabled. *Lineage:* coach's ALL ABOUT SQUATS; Westside [S46]; mixed evidence [S25]; AEL [S26].
32. **Units and data hygiene.** Store reps as text ('10/10' = per side, '6-8' = range) and never as numbers or dates; give athletic work its own units (contacts, throws, cuts, metres, seconds). *Lineage:* observed Google Sheets date corruption across both workbooks.
