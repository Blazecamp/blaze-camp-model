# Coach round-2 answers (AUTHORITATIVE)

Collected 2026-10-08 from the questionnaire page (round 2 + the two leftover round-1 questions). Each: question, what we PROPOSED, coach chip, coach words. RIGHT with no text = the proposal is approved as his rule.

## R2-VOL — Your volume math: approve or fix

### R2-VOL-01 — Each week the plan grows by 3, 5 or 7 % depending on how the person's week went. Does this table match how you'd adjust a client?
- **We proposed:** Effort mostly 1-6 (too easy): +7 % (+5 % if life was harder). Effort 7-8 (tough / perfect): +5 % (+3 % if life was harder). Effort 9: hold (+3 % only if life was better than usual). Any '10 - I'm done': hold and remove 1 set from that day. Life 'much harder' (sick, travel, no sleep): hold. Never more than 7 % a week; repeat the week if they did under half the sessions.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R2-VOL-02 — 'Leave 1 in the tank' is very hard, but your effort menu calls 9 'That was terrible'. Should clients rate effort against what the set asked for?
- **We proposed:** Yes: '8 - That was perfect' means 'I hit the target the plan gave me'. 9 = harder than planned, 10 = failed or wrecked. Otherwise a client doing exactly the 1 rep in reserve you ask for logs 9 and the plan never grows.
- **Coach verdict:** 🟡 PARTLY
- **Coach says:** 10 should be like, "That was the max I can do, not a fail." We can add a fail with it if we need to, but 10 should be like, "I can't do any more than this. I gave it all I had, but it was successful."

### R2-VOL-03 — If someone never checks in, what weekly step should their plan use?
- **We proposed:** 5 % for most people. 3 % for people who are stressed, sleep under 6 hours, have a hard physical job, are 60+, are coming back from 6+ months off, or have pain. 7 % only after check-ins show they handle it. Advanced lifters default to 3 % so the hardest weeks land at the end.
- **Coach verdict:** (no chip)
- **Coach says:** No, if they don't check in, the plan doesn't change. There is no data to go off of.

### R2-VOL-04 — Should small muscles (biceps, triceps, side delts) start the same as big muscles, or a bit lower?
- **We proposed:** A bit lower: beginner 8, intermediate 12, advanced 15 sets a week (big muscles 10 / 15 / 20). Rear delts 4 / 6 / 8 of their own sets. Reason: it fits 5-7 exercises a session, and arms also grow from presses and rows.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, that's correct, but 8 sets a week is probably just barely maintenance. Essentially, the little muscles also grow the same idea.

### R2-VOL-05 — How should glutes count on squats, lunges and deadlifts? And thrusters?
- **We proposed:** Squats, lunges, deadlifts and RDLs = a quarter set for glutes (your helper rule, and your 'mostly quads' on lunges). Hip thrusts, bridges and KB swings = 1 full set for glutes. Thrusters = 1 set quads + half a set shoulders (your thruster answer wins over the 0.25 rule, like dips). Say so if deadlifts and RDLs should count half a set for glutes instead.
- **Coach verdict:** (no chip)
- **Coach says:** Yeah, but kettlebell swings are not glutes. They are hip-dominant, but at the speed they are swinging like that, it doesn't really become a resistance movement-building muscle work. It may be for athletics, but kettlebell swings are mostly supposed to be used for the ballistic movements, not for resistance training. And yes, that sounds like the math is correct on the way to count for compound movements that include glutes. A quarter of the time:
  - A thruster should be 1 set for squats.
  - Half a set for shoulders.
  - A quarter set for glutes.
  That's it, but that's the idea. Yeah, that's good math.

### R2-VOL-06 — What's the most weekly sets a muscle should reach in one 12-week plan?
- **We proposed:** Non-priority: beginner 14, intermediate 22, advanced 25. Priority muscle: beginner 18, intermediate 26-28, advanced 30. While 1-2 priorities are pushed, the other muscles grow at most 3 % a week. The next 12-week cycle starts 5 % higher with the ceiling 1 set higher (never above 25, or 30 for a priority).
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, but this is all customizable. I don't think anybody needs less than 10 sets a week per muscle, but yeah, that sounds about right. More than 30 sets per week of a muscle group should probably be for a very advanced person. Maybe we can add a pro level where some muscles can grow out of that if the person is really adamant about growing that muscle, but I don't think they're going to need around more than that. We still have the availability to add it on, but not necessarily a need. I think most people train around that: 20 to 30 sets per muscle per week. I believe so, but you can do some research and figure it out.

### R2-VOL-07 — Deload is 'about half the sets'. Half of which week, and when does it happen?
- **We proposed:** Half of the current (hardest) week, same exercises, 3+ reps in reserve. No fixed deload week inside a 12-week plan: it comes when check-ins say so (two hold weeks in a row, effort 9-10 two weeks, or pain on 2+ exercises), and week 1 of the next cycle is a deload.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** No maintenance weeks are supposed to be at the end of a block, or if the athlete or coach demands a program of maintenance. It should never be thrown in the middle of a block. No matter what the athlete puts, they need to be able to manage their block and complete it. If they can't complete it, they should cancel the block and start a maintenance phase, but then, when they're done with maintenance, they should start a whole new block.

### R2-VOL-08 — When day A happens twice a week, where does a new set go? And where does a small-muscle bonus exercise go?
- **We proposed:** Alternate: Monday's first exercise, then Thursday's first exercise, so both days stay even. A bonus exercise (2 sets) goes on the optional day as a Focus slot; with no optional day, at the end of the shortest training day.

## R2-SES — Sessions and weeks

### R2-SES-01 — 'Load the spine once a week': one squat day and one deadlift day (two days), or squat and deadlift on the same day?
- **We proposed:** Two different days: heavy squat Monday, heavy deadlift Friday. Tuesday a non-spinal hinge (hip thrust, back extension, reverse hyper). Thursday leg press or belt squat.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R2-SES-02 — Which lifts count as loading the spine?
- **We proposed:** Count: barbell back, front, zercher, overhead and Smith squats; barbell or trap-bar deadlifts and RDLs; good mornings. Don't count: leg press, belt squat, hip thrust, back extension, reverse hyper, single-leg DB work, Olympic lifts, loaded jumps. Goblet squat, hack squat and pendulum: fine on the second day if not sore. Powerlifters may add a light technique squat or deadlift.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R2-SES-03 — When core opens the session before jumps and Olympic lifts, should it stay light?
- **We proposed:** Yes: 2 exercises x 2 sets, short of failure, as a warm-up. Hard core finishers only when the client moves core to the end.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, they can always add supersets of core to any of the exercises that they want to. Sometimes coaches like to do that. They like to give a person their exercise. For example, they'll start with core and then move to other things, and then, when the person is ending, they like to superset with core or superset or monster set with different exercises. It should be optional to be thrown anywhere, but yeah, just like this is fine.

### R2-SES-04 — Your cap for field athletes: 'max maybe 12' in one answer, '12 to 14' in another. Is it 12 plus the 2 core exercises?
- **We proposed:** Yes: up to 12 exercises plus the 2-exercise core block (14 total). Warm-up drills never count, and a superset of two small isolations counts as one.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R2-SES-05 — You use 3-4 exercises per muscle a day, but full-body days only hold 5-7 exercises. On full-body days, OK to give each muscle 1-2 exercises?
- **We proposed:** Yes: 1-2 per muscle on full-body days (volume comes from training it 2-3 times a week, Focus slots and bonus exercises); 3-4 per muscle on split days (push/pull/legs, upper/lower).
- **Coach verdict:** 🟡 PARTLY
- **Coach says:** No, full-body days need to have the same 2-4 exercises per muscle that day, so it might be a little longer day. It could start really short, but because you're doing more volume throughout the week, there's never one exercise, so from 2 all the way to 4

### R2-SES-06 — Aesthetics clients get 4 aerobic sessions, but your sheet says never the same colour twice. What is the 4th?
- **We proposed:** A second easy Orange (long, low). The long walks after workouts are extra, 20-30 minutes easy. All aesthetics / physique clients get the 4, not only competitive bodybuilders.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, but also adjustable. You can always say, "I need more cardio," and then it just adds it to the program. Where I need less cardio, it takes away from the program, so it can always be adjustable.

## R2-ATH — Athletes

## R2-PER — Plans, the year and testing

### R2-PER-01 — Your yearly plan, as we understood it. Right?
- **We proposed:** Jan-Mar: hard work + more cardio, start cutting (sets stay at the starting level). Apr-Jun: summer shape, keep losing fat while building a little muscle. Jul-Sep: maintain at about 70 % of the volume. Oct to mid-Dec: build muscle, less cardio. Last 2 weeks of December: automatic maintenance.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** This is correct, but this is for the general population that wants to just stay in shape and wants to have some type of planning for how the year works (because they can just understand easier how to adjust their nutrition and their lifestyles and all of that). This is for gen pop and general staying healthy and in shape type of life.

### R2-PER-02 — Should the year follow the user's own calendar?
- **We proposed:** Yes: use the user's hemisphere for summer and let them mark their own vacation and holiday weeks (Christmas, Semana Santa, Thanksgiving...), which switch to maintenance automatically. Athletes follow their season instead.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, that's correct. They can set it up however they want, and they can adjust their year as well. They can just switch it around if they don't like the blocks the way they're set up, or if they want to add something, change something, or change the variety (more cardio, less cardio). They can tune it up if they want to tune it up a little bit better for themselves, how they like it.
  
  Somebody might want to go into deep winter, so really just take away ball cardio and just ramp it up to powerlifting style. They can do that. They're just tuning it, equalizing it to their needs. This is, again, for an option to be able to modify for gen-pop.

### R2-PER-03 — 8-week plan: 4 weeks getting used to it + 4 driving hard, or 3 + 5? And does 'start hard' mean more sets?
- **We proposed:** 4 + 4. 'Start hard' = about 10 % more sets than the normal start, with the weekly step capped at 5 % in weeks 1-4.
- **Coach verdict:** ❌ NOT RIGHT
- **Coach says:** No, the 8-week plan should gradually go up like steps up a stairway. That's how every program should be. It should be like steps up a stairway. In the beginning, the first 2, 3, 4 weeks, the steps are a little bit smaller, but they're gradually going up. When they get to that halfway point and upwards, the steps get a little bit higher, but not too much. It's just a gradual step, a ramp-up, and that goes for every plan.

### R2-PER-04 — 12-week plan: weeks 1-3 getting used to it (1-4 for beginners or people coming back, 1-2 for advanced), drive to week 9, hardest weeks 10-12. OK?
- **We proposed:** Yes, with a 3 % max weekly step during the getting-used-to-it weeks.
- **Coach verdict:** 🟡 PARTLY
- **Coach says:** Yeah, on a 12-week program, the last 4 weeks are going to be brutal. Again, like a stairway: steps up, steps up, and week by week, it just gets gradually worse. I mean worse, meaning better, more volume.

### R2-PER-05 — 'Start at a 2 and end at an 11': advanced lifters are already at the 25-set ceiling. Where does 'one level higher' come from for them?
- **We proposed:** From heavier loads, intensifiers (myo sets, supersets) and a rotating priority muscle that can go up to 30 sets, not from more sets on everything.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** When I say started at 2 and ended at 11, I mean the amount of volume that they're putting themselves at as far as level, not the amount of sets. It feels like if you do RPE, you start feeling like a 2, and then that's how you start your program, and then you're gradually going up that ladder. The last week might feel like an 11, like you're doing more than what you usually do. That's how you should end up in your block.
  
  I'm just talking about how it feels and what level you're starting at. By the time you're done with your block, ideally, you've pushed yourself a little bit past your limits, which is what an 11 really would mean. I'm just talking about overall limitations and what you usually do as far as volume and total loading and all of that.

### R2-PER-06 — Is pre-season the same as the 4-6-week 'season change' maintenance block?
- **We proposed:** Yes: 4-6 weeks at about 60 % of off-season sets with more speed and power, ending with a 1-2-week taper before the first game.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, but that should always be able to be modified. You should be able to pick how many weeks you're going to do with maintenance. When you're doing those weeks, you can add more weeks so that you can keep going on maintenance, or it just stops and says:
  - Do you want to keep going on maintenance?
  - Do you want to change your plan into a better program?
  - Do you want to change your plan into a block of training?
  Kind of thing.

### R2-PER-07 — Testing: is this the menu and timing?
- **We proposed:** Ticked at block start: strength (beginners a 5-8 rep max; intermediate and up a 3-rep max or work up to a 1-rep max), cardio (1-mile run or an erg time trial), speed and agility (10-30 m sprint, 5-10-5, L-test), jumps, body weight. Tested in the last week instead of that week's top sets. Reminders at block start, 7 days before and the day before.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Ideally, that's correct. You should be able to test before and then test at the end. We should be giving value to people, and they should not test during the weeks, during the block. They should test before they start their block, or at the beginning, and at the end.
  
  The problem might be that some people are going to want to test their one-rep maxes at the beginning of a block, and they're not ready for it. If they haven't finished a block of training to be able to test that, then they should never start from zero testing their maxes. They can start from zero if they're starting from zero at a 5-rep max. That way, it's safer, or a 3-rep max. That way, it's safer, but you should not go from not training to a one-rep max test. That should never be like that, or a one-mile run speed test. Things like that. They should try to test at 80%, 90%, but not at 100%.
