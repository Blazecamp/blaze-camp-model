## R4-SES — Session size (no more time-fitting)

### R4-SES-01 — Should the form still ask 'how long do you want to train'?
- **We proposed:** No. Drop it. The plan shows an estimate like 'about 60-75 minutes' on each day, as information only. Nothing is ever cut or changed because of time.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Drop it. We don't need it.  Sometimes it might be 45 minutes. Sometimes they can take off exercises and make it faster. Sometimes they can add exercises and make it longer. We don't need to be counting their time. We can talk about averages, but no need for it.

### R4-SES-02 — How many exercises per session, by type of client? (Not counting warm-up or the 2 core exercises.)
- **We proposed:** General fitness / fat loss: 5-6. Aesthetics / bodybuilding: 6-8. Strength: 5-6. Bootcamp: 5-7 stations. Field athletes off-season: up to 12 (jumps/throws + Olympic + lifts). Older adults 60+: 5-6. Everyone can add or remove exercises.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, this is correct as long as it's logical. This looks good. Adjust according to general fitness and fat loss. You can probably go from 5 to 7. They can add 1 if they want to. This is just our recommendation. These are templates that we're giving them for them to add or take away, but those are good recommendations. Just make sure you double-check with whatever data is out there and adjust accordingly within those parameters that I gave you.

### R4-SES-03 — Someone trains only 2 days a week. With your exercise counts, not every muscle can get 10+ sets a week. What gives?
- **We proposed:** Every big muscle still gets trained, with fewer weekly sets (about 6-8 instead of 10), and the plan says so and suggests a 3rd day. We do NOT drop whole muscles, and we do NOT pile on more exercises. (Your earlier answer was 'train fewer muscles directly'. Tell us which you want.)
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** That sounds right, but if you find that there is something better that we can give them, then good. If not, if this sounds pretty logical, it makes sense, and the data backs it up, the science backs it up, then we leave it there. It's not a problem. People can add or remove or adjust accordingly. That's fine. We can maybe give them a small recommendation, but it's only a recommendation.

### R4-SES-04 — When a session hits its exercise count, what gets left out first?
- **We proposed:** Always keep: the 2 core exercises, the main lifts, and the athletic block for athletes. Leave out first: extra accessories for small muscles, then extra jump/throw stations, then conditioning finishers (moved to the optional day).
- **Coach verdict:** 🟡 PARTLY
- **Coach says:** That's something for the athlete to adjust. There could be a question of what's more important here, and then they get to pick what they want to keep or what they want to take away. It's something that they can choose whatever they want.

### R4-SES-05 — On a full-body day, how many exercises does each body part get? You gave us two rules that don't fit together there: (1) a normal session has about 5-6 exercises, and (2) every muscle gets 2-4 exercises a day, never just 1. Six muscles x 2 exercises would be 12 exercises. Which one wins?
- **We proposed:** Count by AREA on full-body days: legs, push (chest, shoulders, triceps) and pull (back, biceps). Each area gets 2 exercises. Example day: squat + lunge, bench press + shoulder press, row + pulldown = 6 exercises. So chest alone may get 1 exercise that day, but 'push' gets 2. On split days (push day, pull day, leg day) every muscle still gets 2-4. The other option: full-body days are longer (10-12 exercises) so every single muscle gets 2.
- **Coach verdict:** ❌ NOT RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, usually, full-body days are going to do something like 6 exercises. The template is usually 6 exercises for the full-body days because we do that A-day/B-day of upper/lower, and I'll explain what that means. We don't necessarily do full-body days (push, pull, legs). That's when you only have, let's say, 3 days a week of training or 2 days a week of training.
  We usually use the full-body days: day A, day B, upper, lower. We do:
  - Monday: day A
  - Tuesday: day B
  - Wednesday: a rest
  - Thursday: day A again
  - Friday: day B
  - Saturday and Sunday: rest
  Something like that. The rest can be cardio, or the rest can be different things. If you look at the program, I've done that and I've put that together so that we understand those templates.
  Sometimes people, on Wednesdays and on Saturdays, for example, will do their cardio. They'll do their accessory movements, like their bonuses, things they want to focus on, but that's how we do it. Ideally, those days that are full-body (upper/lower) can run anywhere from 6 exercises to 8 exercises, and that's already a great training day. The days that they do 8 exercises, they're just going to be adding an extra small muscle exercise at the end, or a core, or something like that, but the template needs to be sound science. Remember that the exercises for full-body days are going to be:
  - Day A: back, quads, and biceps. It's going to be more pull- and knee-dominant movements.
  - Day B: push with hip-dominant movements. Let's say, for example, chest, shoulders, and tricep with hamstrings and glutes.
  That's how we design full bodies. We don't design full bodies only with push, pull, and legs. This is the model that I believe is the greatest one.
  We have:
  - Push, pull, legs.
  - The full-body upper/lower.
  On the upper/lower days:
  - Day A: everything that has to do with pulling, bicep, and back, for example, and quad dominance (lunges, squats, things like that).
  - Day B: everything that has to do with pushing exercises (chest, shoulders, tricep) and hamstrings and glutes or hip-dominant movements, and includes leg curls as well.
  On Day A, you include extensions as well. We have those two options: push, pull, legs, and the upper/lower days, Day A and Day B. You need to adjust your full-body days to that.
  When it's Day A and B, for example, with those upper/lower days, you can see it in the programming in that Google Drive that I gave you. Those full-body days are usually going to be around 6 exercises:
  - Back movement
  - Knee-dominant movement, bilateral, symmetrical
  - Back movement
  - Knee-dominant movement, unilateral
  - Bicep
  - Leg extension
  Day A is going to look something like:
  - Horizontal pull, bilateral, symmetrical
  - Knee-dominant movement, bilateral, symmetrical (a squat)
  - Vertical pull, like a lat pulldown
  - Unilateral knee-dominant movement, like a lunge
  - Bicep curls
  - Leg extensions
  That will be a great full-body day. I hope you understand that.
  Day B, for example, would be something like: horizontal pushing, like bench press or machine chest press. We're going to go into:
  - Bilateral, symmetrical hip-dominant movement, like a deadlift, a hip extension, or it could be a hip thrust
  - Vertical push, like shoulder press or an incline press. That is more vertical than horizontal.
  - Unilateral hip-dominant movement, like a stagger-stance deadlift with dumbbells, a single-leg hip extension, or a glute bridge
  - Tricep extensions
  - Leg curls
  I hope you understand that for full-body days.

## R4-PRG — Progression

### R4-PRG-01 — The top set number for a muscle: reach it exactly in the last week, or is arriving a week or two early and holding it there fine?
- **We proposed:** Reach it in the last week when the math allows. Arriving one week early and holding with more reps or load is fine. Never earlier than that.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R4-PRG-02 — You said 'increase faster if that's the need'. In extreme cases (a 4-week block with a big target) that can mean +50% sets in one week. Limit or no limit?
- **We proposed:** No hard limit, but the plan prints a clear note when any week adds more than about 15% ('this week jumps more than usual: adjust if needed').
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R4-PRG-03 — How should rep and load progress be written, since there are no check-ins?
- **We proposed:** As a rule the person applies themselves: '3 sets of 8-12. When you hit 12 on every set with 1-2 reps left in the tank, add weight next time and start again at 8.' Not as a promise like 'week 3: 12 reps'.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R4-PRG-04 — When someone finishes a block and starts the next one, where do their sets start?
- **We proposed:** A little above where the last block started (about 5% more, rounded to whole sets), never above the level's top number. If they're already at the top, sets stay and progress comes from load and reps.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, and it has to be logical.
