# Blaze Camp: Pre-Release Model Review

**First deliverable only | 9 October 2026 | Approximately 15 minutes**

**Verdict:** A substantial, recognizable coaching system with a strong exercise vocabulary and unusually good source traceability. Its main release risk is not missing exercises: it is presenting precise coaching defaults as universally appropriate doses, especially when those defaults compete with time, sport practice, or individual capacity. Publish it as an explicit, configurable coaching philosophy, not a scientifically validated universal prescription.

**Scope and method.** Reviewed the source model at [commit c13cbaa](https://github.com/Blazecamp/blaze-camp-model/tree/c13cbaa38317ab58c0e6e44d4c41ed858991f592), beginning with [CODEX_BRIEF](../CODEX_BRIEF.md), then [README](../README.md), [BRAIN](../BLAZE_CAMP_BRAIN.md), [generator specification](../generator_spec_v1.json), the synthesis documents/structured catalogs, and [exercise-contribution documentation](../docs/adding-exercises.md). Authority is [round 3](../coach_answers_round3.md) > [round 2](../coach_answers_round2.md) > [full answers](../coach_answers_full.md) > [chat statements](../coach_chat_statements.md). Excluded `engine/`, `model/`, `tests/`, `samples/`, `tools/`, and `design/`; this is **not an implementation audit**. No model files changed.

Web research covered reviews, trials, position stands, original coaching-system documentation, databases, and practitioner communities through the review date. This is a critical narrative review, not a registered systematic review or an exhaustive evaluation of every sport. **Strong** means convergent evidence for a principle, not proof of Blaze's exact number; **moderate/limited** denotes population or methodological limitations; **practice** means expert opinion/tradition. Commercial systems supply useful design comparisons, not independent proof of superiority. Where full text was unavailable, conclusions are limited to the accessible abstract. Every proposed departure from an authoritative answer is marked **for discussion with the coach**.

## 1. The Model in My Own Words

Blaze Camp combines a movement classification system, a muscle-volume accounting system, and sport-specific scheduling. It builds an entire editable plan once. There are **no check-ins, stored personal data, or automatic adaptation after generation**. People can change their exported plan and use the printed adjustment guidance; a new block requires a new build. That is an intentional product boundary, not missing tracking functionality.

The main exercise families are athletic, resistance, and control/core, extended by conditioning, functional carries/drags/slings, and preparation/testing. Resistance uses knee-dominant and hip-dominant bilateral/unilateral patterns, separate knee flexion, horizontal/vertical pushing and pulling, and muscle-specific accessories. Core distinguishes thorax, pelvis, and both, across planes and static/dynamic actions. Athletic work covers triple extension/Olympic derivatives, jumps/landings, throws, sprinting, and change of direction. Conditioning has Blue, Green, Orange, and lactate-clearance/circuit prescriptions.

Exercises carry laterality, plane, muscle contribution, skill/stability, load position, equipment alternatives, constraints, and population flags. **Exercise difficulty is separate from the client's volume level.** Levels guide rather than prohibit selection. The five-point constraint scale describes supported/fixed versus free/reactive work. Davidson-style position/rotation zones and contralateral/ipsilateral loading are additional coaching lenses, not interchangeable with levels. The source catalog contains **939 records**, including **633 typed exercises**, plus drills, complexes, generic choices, prescriptions, modifiers, and retired/out-of-scope entries. **31 records are proposed additions**; catalog presence does not mean approval as a default.

Hard resistance sets generally use 5-30 repetitions and 1-3 repetitions in reserve (RIR). Main movers receive 1.0 set and helpers 0.25, with explicit exceptions. A unilateral set performed on both sides is one set per relevant muscle, not two. Athletic and conditioning work receive no muscle-set credit. Weekly floors use groups, including hips and combined shoulders, while individual muscle information remains useful. Big-muscle starts are 10/15/20 for L1/L2/L3, with ordinary ceilings 14/22/25; shoulders/arms have different starts, calves one exercise for three sets per leg day, and core a light two-exercise/two-set opener. Goal, season, pain, maintenance, repeated blocks, and up to two Focus groups modify this.

The **stairway** increases planned volume about 3% weekly through the first half and 5% thereafter, scaled to available ceilings; fractional additions become whole sets. Effort drifts from three toward one RIR, with late intensifiers. Representative 12-week big-muscle paths are 10 to 14, 15 to 22, and 20 to 25. Exercise blocks stay stable for roughly 4-6 weeks. Eight-/12-week plans, maintenance, annual plans, event preparation, and seasonal plans have different shapes. No deload is inserted inside a training block; an easy week afterward is recommended, not mandatory.

Sessions usually run warm-up, light core, speed/jumps/throws, Olympic work, strength, accessories, conditioning. Priority work, core-position preferences, and fighter conditioning create exceptions. Hip + push and knee + pull are characteristic pairings; horizontal precedes vertical. Counted heavy spinal squat and hinge exposures occur on different days, once each weekly. Sport/position, season, practice/game days, recreational activities, age, and equipment alter selection and dose. There are 127 profiles routing 52 sports and 26 activities, not 127 independently validated programs. When time is insufficient, the model offers more time/days, then reduces directly trained groups and recommends supersets.

**Tuning surface:** intake covers language/units, role/screening, age/body weight, experience/layoff, goal/format, sport/position/level/season, practice/play/event dates, Focus/rep preference, days/minutes, plan/calendar/maintenance/history, tests, exact equipment/loads/space, pain/exclusions, and editing preferences. The [variables model](../synthesis/11_training_variables.md) covers load, speed intent, tempo, ROM/end range, volume, rest/density, effort, frequency, selection/constraint, variation, order, position, load laterality, stance, grip/feet, planes/core, jump phases/contact characteristics, ball mass, conditioning, bilateral share, exercise level, pairing, and axial budget; accommodating resistance is deliberately retired. Coach rule packs can modify defaults but not locked philosophy.

## 2. What Is Strong

- **Progressive resistance training, multiple sets, and purposeful effort: strong.** The [2026 ACSM overview of 137 reviews](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/) supports resistance training across strength, size, power, and physical function. The [IUSCA stand, including Schoenfeld and Helms](https://journal.iusca.org/index.php/Journal/article/view/81), supports organizing hypertrophy around sufficient volume and effort. This supports the foundation, not every floor or ceiling.
- **Direct/indirect set accounting: moderate.** Recognizing overlap is more defensible than treating presses, rows, and isolation work as unrelated. [Pelland, Remmert, Robinson et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w) support fractional accounting and diminishing returns. The precise credits still need caution.
- **Stable exercises with deliberate variation: moderate.** [Kassiano et al.'s eight-study review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/) favors systematic over excessive random variation, although all participants were young men and it establishes no magic four-/six-week interval.
- **Power is trained as a distinct quality: moderate to strong.** Landing progressions, fast intent, and separate power prescriptions fit [Ramirez-Campillo et al.'s plyometric meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10115703/) and the [NSCA weightlifting stand by Comfort, Haff, Suchomel, Stone et al.](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/weighlifting-for-sports-performance.pdf). Pulls and simpler derivatives also have a sound rationale without requiring every athlete to master catches. [Suchomel et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25689955/)
- **Machines and practical alternatives belong: moderate.** [Haugen et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426227/) found similar hypertrophy outcomes with machines/free weights and test-specific strength effects. Blaze sensibly avoids making free-weight complexity synonymous with training quality.
- **Seasonal reductions, tapers, and maintenance: supported principles.** [Bosquet et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/) support reducing volume before competition; [Bickel et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131862/) demonstrate maintenance with reduced dosing, with age-related differences. Neither validates every Blaze percentage. Protecting competition days and avoiding redundant endurance work are sensible practices.
- **Traceability and honest boundaries are valuable system design.** Coach-answer IDs, observed/inferred/proposed distinctions, bilingual terminology, explicit exclusions, equipment-aware swaps, and separate client/exercise levels make the model explainable. Preserve these features.

## 3. What Is Missing or Incomplete

### Coverage Before More Exercises

**A time-limited whole-body pathway is the most consequential gap.** The source's own [volume algorithm, section 5.5](../synthesis/12_volume_algorithm_v1.md), shows a 55-year-old pickleball player retaining only hips and pinned calves as directly trained groups at two 45-minute sessions. This is an openly documented consequence, not an engine finding. The ten-set floor plus two-to-four-exercises-per-group rule can crowd out upper-body training. **For discussion with the coach:** offer a low-dose whole-body option before dropping entire major regions. [WHO guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/) prioritizes all major muscle groups, while [Iversen et al.'s time-efficiency review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449772/) explains useful smaller prescriptions. Optimization for hypertrophy and adequacy for general health are different objectives.

**A unified workload view, without new tracking.** Keep separate resistance-set, jump/contact, sprint-distance, throw, conditioning, and sport-practice totals in the generated plan. A zero muscle credit must never imply zero tissue stress. Add planned exposure by region, high-impact versus extensive contacts, actual rest, surface, and unfamiliar eccentric loading. These extend existing tags/budgets; they need not store anything. [Concurrent-training evidence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/) makes timing especially relevant to explosive adaptations.

**Library approval and execution detail.** Nordics, Copenhagen planks, suspension rows, landmine work, and core anti-movement options already exist. Prioritize approval/completion of proposed carries/drags, reactive split-step work, cuff/serratus work, soleus/tibialis work, and neck isometrics, rather than duplicating them. Functional posterior-oblique/lateral categories remain sparsely populated. Add useful braking-to-stop and opponent-cued progressions where approved coverage is thin. Every entry needs setup, measurable dose units, regression/progression, assistance, side accounting, and quality-stop instructions. Existing Nordics deserve evidence-backed scheduling/adherence guidance, not just a name in the library: prevention programs including them reduced hamstring injuries in a [large meta-analysis](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/53/21/1362).

**Equipment completeness is a scope choice.** Better specify safe home pulling anchors, assistance, microloading, machine fit, sled surface/friction, and usable running/deceleration space. Flywheels and adaptive equipment could expand future scope but are not release necessities. Bands/chains/releasers and V-squat are intentional exclusions. **For discussion with the coach:** revisiting bands could improve low-cost/accessibility coverage; ACSM supports elastic resistance as a viable modality, not a requirement.

### Sport and Population Depth

More sport names are lower priority than validated needs analyses. Suggested coverage work:

| Sport family | What should distinguish its prescription | Evidence and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Soccer, basketball, volleyball, football, rugby | Actual practice jumps/sprints/collisions, braking, reactive decisions, position, tournament congestion | [COD angle/velocity review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30094799/); [NSCA football position-specific resources](https://www.nsca.com/education/journals/nsca-coach/football-issue-5-4/). Exact doses remain context dependent. |
| Baseball/softball, overhead and racket sports | Throw/serve exposure, shoulder/elbow demands, rotational power, deceleration; pickleball is not automatically tennis | [Overarm-training review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11550301/), [tennis review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40605097/). Transfer differs by movement and population. |
| Sprinting/jumping | Acceleration versus maximum velocity, full recovery, direction, contact behavior, approach speed | [Haugen et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31754845/); [Morin/Samozino](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26694658/). Optional profiling requires reliable measurement, not mandatory technology. |
| Running, cycling, rowing, swimming/triathlon | Strength that complements endurance; event/stroke, starts/turns, and competition taper | Reviews in [running](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38165636/), [cycling](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40632222/), [rowing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32216524/), [swimming](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39129754/). Do not infer equal effects or identical doses. |
| Combat, golf, ice hockey, HYROX/CrossFit | Weight-class/round demands; swing skill; skating-specific transfer; station and running demands under fatigue | [Combat review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36834211/), [golf associations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38424374/), [hockey testing review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37841888/), [HYROX's research program](https://hyrox.com/the-science-of-hyrox/). Associations and organizer reports are not causal program validation. |

Label nearest-profile routes, such as surfing to freestyle skiing, as **provisional analogies**, not demonstrated equivalents. A complete strength-and-conditioning model need not replace the sport coach or generate full marathon, swim, or technical practice plans.

**Population boundaries need equal clarity.** Youth need supervision and movement competence, not just age/maturity tags; older adults need functional capacity, balance, and suitable power options. See [youth consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24055781/) and [NSCA older-adult position statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31343601/). Pregnancy/postpartum, disability, frailty, osteoporosis, significant chronic disease, and return from surgery/injury need explicit supported/coach-only/refer boundaries. Existing screening should own these decisions; no sex-based dosing or lifestyle questionnaire is required. Expanding intake or clinical scope is **for discussion with the coach**, not a silent addition. [WHO population guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128/)

**Testing needs operational standards.** Specify familiarization, warm-up, timing method, surface, trials, rest, and normal measurement variation. A 5-10-5 test measures preplanned COD, not reactive agility. [Nimphius et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26982972/) also show why total COD time can conceal straight-line speed. Baseline/end-only testing can remain; specify recovery around the end test so the hardest week's fatigue does not obscure improvement.

## 4. What Is Debatable or Weakly Supported

**All departures below are for discussion with the coach, not model errors.**

| Rule | Best case for it | Evidence limit / decision needed |
|---|---|---|
| Ten-set floor; 15-20 as maintenance-to-growth; advanced starts/caps | Multiple sets and higher volumes can produce more growth. | Neither [ACSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/) nor [Pelland et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w) establishes ten as a universal minimum or 15-20 as maintenance. Benefits occur below this; returns diminish. Youth, general health, and athletic performance should not inherit a bodybuilding optimization target automatically. |
| Every block climbs; 3%/5%; less-than-10% weekly limit | Predictable, gradual loading and acclimation are coherent coaching practices. | [Enes et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37796222/) found greater strength gains with larger set progressions but no statistically clear between-group hypertrophy difference; [Barsuhn et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39665246/) found gains while maintaining previous volume. Neither validates Blaze's curve. A sub-10% increase is not an injury guarantee; even the running [10% rule lacks universal support](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528699/), and running distance is not lifting sets. |
| Helper 0.25; glutes 0.25 in every squat/hinge; MYO = two sets | Simple, transparent bookkeeping avoids double counting. | Pelland compared indirect credits of 0, 0.5, and 1: its best-fitting 0.5 did **not** directly test 0.25. Do not replace one universal constant with another. [Squat/hip-thrust MRI research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37877099/) found similar glute growth in that novice protocol, challenging a literal fourfold stimulus interpretation. MYO equivalence is a convention, not a measured conversion. |
| RIR 3 to 1 plus intensifiers; no in-block deload | Near-failure work can support hypertrophy; mandatory scheduled rests are not established. | [Robinson et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2) find different relationships for strength versus hypertrophy, with estimated RIR and substantial uncertainty. [Coleman et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/) found no hypertrophy benefit and less strength gain with a week off; that does not establish that reduced-load weeks or earlier recovery are never useful. Preserve manual stop/repeat/rebuild options. |
| 80-90% unilateral for COD sports; exact plyometric/ballistic shares | Both laterality and contact behavior create useful specificity. | [Unilateral/bilateral meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35959319/) supports task-specific benefits, not these percentages. Ballistic and plyometric are overlapping scientific categories; slow stretch-shortening actions can still be plyometric. Combining throws and jumps in one percentage can change the apparent leg prescription without changing leg work. |
| Contact ceilings 80/100/120; fixed receive-create weeks; fixed ball masses | Practical ceilings and landing-first teaching can organize exposure. | [Plyometric evidence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10115703/) supports benefits more firmly than a universal contact dose. NSCA itself describes [much lower volumes for high-quality drills](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/plyometric-exercises/). Count low hops, depth jumps, unilateral landings, and throws separately. Technique/readiness and output quality should complement calendar progression. |
| Core first; horizontal first; hip + push/knee + pull | Consistency, an unfatiguing primer, and alternating regions can work well. | [Exercise-order meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32077380/) mainly supports putting the strength priority first, not a universal plane order. [Superset evidence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12011898/) supports time savings with greater perceived exertion, not automatic VO2max improvement or a universal 35% saving. |
| Two counted spinal days; constrained machines for pain; rotation-bias swaps | Limiting repeated heavy exposures and selecting tolerable alternatives are reasonable practices. | Olympic lifts, loaded jumps, and unilateral DB work are not literally spine-unloaded. Treat exclusions as a scheduling convention. [McGill's biomechanical framework](https://www.backfitpro.com/medical-scientific-articles/2010/%5B70%5DMcGill%2CS.M.%282010%29Core-training%5BJ.Strength-and-Condit.%5D.pdf) supports attention to load/control, but [clinical synthesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31775556/) does not support simple posture-to-pain causation. Davidson/Hartman lenses are coaching hypotheses, not validated diagnostic or pain-treatment algorithms. [Hartman's own material emphasizes individualization](https://billhartmanpt.com/tag/exercises-for-shoulder-health/). |
| French contrast without inside rest | Dense contrast work can be purposeful. | [PAPE meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1202789/full) found immediate heavy-to-jump transitions can depress acute output; longer recovery often helps. Acute PAPE is not identical to long-term adaptation. |
| Olympic work for athletic clients; retained 4 x 8 at 75% to 4 x 4 at 90% derivative ramp | Powerful triple extension and scalable derivatives are useful tools. | Round 2 already limits full squat lifts to 3-5 reps and makes percentages conditional on a tested lift/coach choice. Preserve that correction. Still define the percentage reference and stop sets when output deteriorates; no single rep/load curve fits catches, pulls, DBs, and KBs. [NSCA weightlifting stand](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/weighlifting-for-sports-performance.pdf) |

Likewise, small-muscle/high-rep preferences, seasonal physique quarters, exact maintenance factors, and 48-hour scheduling buffers are configurable heuristics. Age 60 alone is not a capability threshold: conservative defaults and the approved opt-in need competence guidance. [NSCA older-adult stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31343601/) ROM and muscle-length tags are useful, but emerging [long-length evidence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40570881/) does not justify forcing every exercise into one maximal-stretch prescription.

## 5. Structure Review

**Sound architecture; incomplete separation of meaning, policy, and evidence.** The taxonomy should describe a movement; coaching policy should decide whether, when, and how to use it. An exercise's name, historical spreadsheet placement, or branded system should not silently determine its dose or safety status.

**What other systems contribute.** These are comparisons, not proposed wholesale replacements:

| System | Useful comparison with Blaze |
|---|---|
| [Davidson's movement taxonomy](https://akademija.vigor.si/event/pat-davidson-vigorground) | Pattern, stance, plane, load, and velocity form a useful descriptive grammar. A coherent classification does not itself establish which dose produces the best outcome. |
| [RP](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth) | Volume landmarks and accumulation are similar; RP explicitly makes recovery/response part of the framework. Its commercial app is not evidence for a fixed universal stairway. |
| [Juggernaut](https://www.jtsstrength.com/scientific-principles-of-weightlifting/) / [5/3/1](https://www.jimwendler.com/blogs/jimwendler-com/101065094-5-3-1-for-a-beginner) | Explicit specificity/fatigue management; conservative training maxima and simple progression. Useful counterexamples to mandatory increases in set count. |
| [Westside/conjugate](https://www.westside-barbell.com/blogs/the-blog/applying-the-conjugate-method) / [Triphasic](https://triphasictraining.com/) | Distinct maximal/dynamic/repeated-effort purposes, and eccentric/isometric/concentric emphases. Blaze can represent these qualities without adopting bands, maximal attempts, or claims of system superiority. |
| [ALTIS](https://altis.world/product/altis-need-for-speed/) / [NSCA](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/needs-analysis-for-injury-prevention) | Event-specific mechanics, coaching observation, and needs analysis should precede a universal athletic template. Public material supports this comparison; paid curricula were not audited. |
| [CrossFit](https://library.crossfit.com/free/pdf/CFJ_English_Level1_TrainingGuide.pdf) / [HYROX](https://hyrox.com/training-partner/hybrid-program/) | Preserve workout stimulus through scaling; distinguish mixed-modal conditioning from strength/power practice and sport preparation. Neither brand validates Blaze's exact weekly schedule. |

Practitioner communities raise useful feasibility questions: [SBS users discuss fitting programs into limited time](https://www.reddit.com/r/StrongerByScience/comments/1ohn1al), and [weightroom program reviews](https://www.reddit.com/r/weightroom/comments/s1omsw) describe tradeoffs between assistance, conditioning, and main lifts. These are self-selected reports with survivorship and reporting bias, not comparative trials. Research authors may also coach, sell books, or apps; for example, [Robinson et al. disclose industry roles](https://crossmark.crossref.org/dialog/?doi=10.1007%2Fs40279-024-02069-2). Judge methods and replication, not credentials or commercial affiliation alone.

**Database/software comparison:** [free-exercise-db's schema](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/blob/main/schema.json) offers simple IDs, muscles, equipment, instructions, and images; Blaze is richer in programming semantics. [wger](https://wger.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/api.html) offers structured exercise access, and its [routine model](https://wger.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/routines.html) separates day/slot/exercise from sets, reps, load, RIR, and rest. [TeamBuildr tags](https://blog.teambuildr.com/3-ways-to-use-tags-in-teambuildr) support coach-defined filtering. Borrow these separations, not their tracking requirements.

**Do not equate free access with open redistribution.** [free-exercise-db](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db) declares public-domain licensing; [wger](https://github.com/wger-project/wger) distinguishes code, data, and documentation licenses. ExerciseDB has differently branded endpoints: the [.dev free API](https://oss.exercisedb.dev/docs) advertises noncommercial use/attribution, while [.io terms](https://exercisedb.io/terms) restrict raw-content redistribution. Record exact provider, version, asset owner, and license before importing. Apply the same provenance review to borrowed complete programs; this is a release diligence item, not a legal determination.

Recommended structure:

- A compact, versioned rule registry: ID, owner, authority, approval status, condition, action, units, default/range, exceptions, precedence, source, evidence confidence, review date. **Approved** and **scientifically well-supported** must be independent fields.
- Separate exercises, variations, complexes, tests, and prescriptions. A variation changing equipment, laterality, support, or safety tags must produce a fully resolved record, not merely a new display name.
- Separate actual working sets, fractional muscle credits, group credits, and fatigue/exposure totals. Never compare studies and model caps without matching counting conventions.
- Resolve already-documented source decisions: cap as target versus limit, short blocks that cannot reach it, rounding/one-set exceptions, and Q1 flat sets versus an always-rising stairway. For example, the documented eight-week L2 big-muscle curve ends at 19, below its nominal 22 ceiling. Cite the coach's eventual decision rather than silently choosing an interpretation.
- Publish an active v1.2 entry point. README still says v1.1/185 answers, and the science-lineage file explicitly retains older material. Make historical text visibly non-normative so coaches and AI agents cannot revive retired check-ins or bands.
- Validate the **model**, before its new engine: hand-worked edge cases with accepted outcomes for short sessions, limited kit, young/older athletes, in-season congestion, no compatible exercise, and conflicting preferences. Include reasons and acknowledged compromises.

## 6. Top 10 Improvements, Ranked by Impact

1. **Add a whole-body, time-limited pathway.** Preserve major-region coverage before specialization; reconsider the combined floor/exercise-count constraints. **For discussion with the coach.** Highest benefit for ordinary users. Evidence: [WHO](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/), [time-efficient training review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449772/), and the model's own section 5.5 example.
2. **Calibrate starts to goal and tolerable dose, not experience alone.** Separate health, hypertrophy, strength, and sport-support ranges; allow lower starting volumes and distinguish ceilings from targets. **For discussion with the coach.** Evidence: [ACSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/), [Pelland](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w); athlete-specific [lower-volume evidence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11747014/) is promising but very low certainty.
3. **Make cumulative workload visible.** Retain zero athletic muscle-set credit but show regional exposure and practice overlap; distinguish conditioning from fresh power work. Evidence: [concurrent-training meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/), [NSCA weightlifting stand](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/weighlifting-for-sports-performance.pdf). No storage/check-ins needed.
4. **Operationalize manual quality and recovery decisions.** Print actionable stop/reduce/repeat/rebuild instructions, especially for painful work, speed loss, and unfamiliar loading. Do not describe the stairway as guaranteed tolerance. Changing compulsory progression/recovery rules is **for discussion with the coach**. Evidence: [NSCA youth consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24055781/), [Coleman](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/), [Barsuhn](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39665246/).
5. **Upgrade athletic dosing beyond ratios.** Separate lower-body contacts, throws, sprints, braking, and reactive agility; add competence criteria and quality-based rest. Exact-ratio/ceiling changes are **for discussion with the coach**. Evidence: [plyometric meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10115703/), [COD review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30094799/), [sprint synthesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31754845/).
6. **Declare population and sport-support boundaries.** Make provisional routes and coach-only/clinical pathways unmistakable. Capability should inform older/youth options; changed age policies are **for discussion with the coach**. Evidence: [older-adult stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31343601/), [WHO](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128/), [NSCA needs analysis](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/needs-analysis-for-injury-prevention).
7. **Expose uncertainty in set credits.** Keep the coach's accounting, but label it a convention; compare sensitivity under plausible alternative credits before changing prescriptions. Changes are **for discussion with the coach**. Evidence: [Pelland](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w), [Plotkin's glute trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37877099/).
8. **Create one active rule/parameter registry.** Resolve documented conflicts, separate approval from evidence strength, and retire stale summaries. Evidence: the source's own pending decisions and [wger's explicit prescription structure](https://wger.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/routines.html); this is a systems recommendation, not an exercise-science finding.
