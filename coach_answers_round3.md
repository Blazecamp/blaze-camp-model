## R3-VOL — Your volume math: the last four numbers

### R3-VOL-01 — You said nobody needs less than 10 sets a week per muscle, and you also approved rear delts at 4 / 6 / 8 sets of their own. How do both fit?
- **We proposed:** Rear delts keep 4 / 6 / 8 sets of their own, and the 10 is counted on the whole shoulders group (side-delt work 10-15 sets + rear-delt work + face pulls, so shoulders sit well above 10). The 10-set floor is for training blocks only: maintenance phases, pre-season and in-season stay at your lower maintenance volume (50-70 %), and a muscle cut back for pain stays cut. Field athletes: the muscles their plan trains (chest, back) go up to 10; arms and side delts stay helper work.
- **Coach verdict:** 🟡 PARTLY
- **Coach says:** Yeah, rear delts are weird because they're part of the shoulder cap, so there are side delts and front delts as well. I just figured the shoulders were what we were talking about. If you put together all the sets of shoulders, it'll equate to more than 10 sets and an accumulation over a period of time during the week. I think the rear delts also get worked when you do back, so that sounds about right. I wouldn't categorize the rear delt as an individual muscle, so it should always be adaptable. People and coaches should always be able to add more rear delts, I guess, if they wanted, but that's the way I look at it.

### R3-VOL-02 — Your approved check-in table had 'life was harder / better' cells, but you also said no questions about sleep, stress or anything else. OK to drop the life cells?
- **We proposed:** Yes: the check-in only asks which sessions were done, how hard they felt and whether anything hurts. Effort mostly 1-6: a bit more next week. 7-8: the planned step. 9: hold. Any 10: hold and 1 set less that day. Under half the sessions: repeat the week. Someone who was sick shows it through missed sessions or high effort, so the plan still reacts.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, we're not going to have check-ins, correct? I want to take away all check-in info. We're only doing outputs, and people can change accordingly.

### R3-VOL-03 — (Re-asking R2-VOL-08 in plain words.) A 4-day plan has the same Day A on Monday and Thursday. When the plan adds one more back set, where does it go? And where do 2 extra biceps sets go when every training day is full?
- **We proposed:** The back set goes to Monday's first back exercise this time and Thursday's first back exercise the next time, so both days stay even. The biceps sets become a short bonus biceps exercise on the optional day (e.g. Saturday); with no optional day, on a day that already trains biceps, never as the only biceps exercise of that day.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R3-VOL-04 — Your stairway in numbers: is this how the weekly steps should feel?
- **We proposed:** Sets grow about 3 % a week until the halfway week and about 5 % after it (12 weeks: weeks 2-6 small steps, 7-12 bigger), and each muscle's steps are sized so it reaches its ceiling exactly in the last week. Example: intermediate big muscle 15 to 22 sets, the last four weeks 19, 20, 21, 22. To make week 1 feel like a 2 and the last week like an 11, effort goes from 3 reps in the tank early, to 2, to 1-2, to 1 + intensifiers in the last week, always inside your 1-3. With a check-in, 'perfect' = the planned step and 'too easy' = the planned step + 2 points (max 7 %): +3 / +5 % in the first half, your +5 / +7 % after it.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, but remember that we can just kick out the program designed already without the input of somebody saying how they feel or whatever. It doesn't really matter. The program will just have its natural algorithm recommendation of growth throughout it, so anybody can just plug in the numbers of what they want as far as weeks and sport or goals. The program would just kick itself out, and then people will adjust accordingly over time.
  
  I want this to be very simple. This works. We're not going to need a lot of inputs for this. I don't want people to be checking back on their phone in their program. I want them to be able to kick out a program, and that's it. That's the program. That's what you have to follow, and the program will tell you to adjust accordingly. It's already a template, and you need to adjust it as you need to.
  
  I guess that's probably the easiest, best way for people to get their program, whether it be coaches, athletes, or general population people. It doesn't matter. This needs to be very simple for them to see the whole thing ahead of time, ideally, and they can adjust ahead of time, ideally. It doesn't need to rely too much on inputs.

## R3-SES — Sessions

### R3-SES-02 — Biggest open decision: when the time someone picks can't fit your minimums (10 sets per muscle, 2-4 exercises per muscle, 3 calf sets per leg day, a climbing stairway), what should give? Example: a pickleball player with 2 x 45 min, or a CrossFit class member with 4 x 60 min.
- **We proposed:** In this order: (1) tell them up front and offer a 3rd day or longer sessions; (2) if they keep their time, train fewer muscles directly (big movers first; arms, calves and rear delts only from compound credit) instead of dropping below the floor on everything; (3) use supersets to save time; (4) the stairway climbs on the muscles that are trained, never flat. Never squeeze 2-4 exercises per muscle into a day that can't hold them.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, this is fine. Recommend doing supersets so that it's a faster workout, but they should be able to get things done.
  
  Also, when there are recreational, general-population people or even athletes, they should be able to turn off that day's athletic training or that day's core training and just focus on resistance. Maybe a pickleball player that played a lot of pickleball has a lot of soreness in their calves, or their connective tissues just don't feel like doing any plies and things like that. Then it could be turned off, or they can delete certain exercises, because an athlete or a coach should be able to modify accordingly.
  
  The options should be:
  - Recommend supersets
  - Adding or removing exercises or blocks within the training session
  
  
  That should be a thing as well.

### R3-SES-01 — Full-body days need 2-4 exercises per muscle, never 1. How do we count a 'muscle', and how does it fit the field-athlete cap of 12 + 2 core?
- **We proposed:** We count your FOCUS groups, with glutes + hamstrings as one 'hips' group (e.g. RDL + hip thrust + leg curl = 3 hip exercises) and front / side / rear delts as 'shoulders'; calves keep one exercise x 3 sets per leg day. Field athletes: 2-4 exercises for each group trained that day (knee + pull day, hip + push day) inside the 12 + 2 core. With your cross pattern that leaves 2 jump / throw stations + 1 Olympic lift, so cross pattern stays the default for most athletes, and your same-pattern athletic template (3-4 jump stations) is used for size-focused and high-level athletes.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yes, and also, when a person is picking their training program, they should be able to choose one of the main muscles that they want to work on the most. The program will then automatically choose extra sets for that one compared to the other one.
  
  For example, if it's a female and she chooses glutes as one of the focus muscles, then the program should bias more toward glutes in the lower body. As much as everything else is balanced, the glutes should have an extra set or two or three. The program should do that. It should automatically understand that you want to have a little bit more volume on that muscle, which is essentially your focus muscle throughout this program.
  
  There could be a main focus muscle 1 and a main focus muscle 2, and that should be enough for the whole thing.

## R3-PER — Blocks and the calendar

### R3-PER-01 — Three calendar details: the easy week between two blocks, holiday weeks, and when the plan offers to stop a block.
- **We proposed:** (1) After a finished block, the easy week at half the sets is an extra week, so the next block keeps all its weeks (12 + 1 = a 13-week quarter in the year plan). (2) Holiday and vacation weeks the user marks are planned between blocks; if someone marks one after a block has started, that block ends there (stop, maintenance, then a new block) unless they remove the mark. (3) With check-ins, the plan only offers 'stop this block' (it never forces it) after 2 hold weeks in a row, 2 weeks of effort 9-10, 2 weeks under half the sessions, or pain on 2+ exercises in a week.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
- **Coach says:** Yeah, maintenance weeks are a recommendation. They don't have to be mandatory, so people can go ahead and start one, or when the block ends, it just ends, and then you have to go back into the app and build whatever you want next.

## R3-ATH — Athletes, swimming and staying athletic

### R3-ATH-01 — Swimming (your first sample plan): are these the right dryland defaults?
- **We proposed:** 3 dryland gym days + 1 optional day (2 for younger or lower-level swimmers), around pool training. 0 extra cardio sessions in the gym by default, because the pool is the aerobic work (the user can add one). Jump work mostly ballistic (25 % plyometric at the end of a block), because starts and turns are single big efforts. Olympic lifts 2-3 days. Shoulder care (external rotation, floor Y/T raises, KB face pulls) every session, never bands. A 2-week dryland taper lined up with the swim coach's taper before the main championship meet; 1 week or none before smaller meets.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R3-ATH-02 — Older clients and pickleball players: what is on by default?
- **We proposed:** From 60: landing drills (step down and stick, no bounce), balance, quick stepping without a hop, light medball throws and fast machine work are on. Low ankle hops, other jumps, Olympic lifts and depth jumps are off and can be switched on with your warning ('Make sure that you have a fitness professional to help you understand what this means'); overhead squats and low-bar squats stay out. Pickleball, padel and tennis players 50+: knee-friendly strength, calf and Achilles work every leg day, rotator-cuff work, and only low-impact jumps (no depth jumps, hurdle hops or bounds) unless switched on.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R3-ATH-03 — When you said gen-pop people want to do 'high rows', did you mean HYROX (8 x 1 km runs, each followed by a workout station)? If yes, is this the plan?
- **We proposed:** Yes, HYROX. 2 strength days (squat + pull, deadlift + push) + 2 hybrid days (run intervals, or 1 km run + 1 station repeated). 4 cardio sessions (Blue, Green, Orange + a second Orange), because running is about half the race. 2 short station circuits a week (sled push / pull, burpee broad jumps, wall balls, farmers carry, sandbag lunges, SkiErg / row) as finishers that do not count as muscle sets. Wall balls with the race ball (never over 20 lb). 40-rep / AMRAP sets only inside these circuits. Jumps 50 % plyometric, like basketball.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R3-ATH-04 — Sports you did not name in round 2: OK to place them with your rule (fast, bouncy contacts = more plyos) like this?
- **We proposed:** Plyometric share at the end of a block: 75 % runners; 60 % lacrosse, field hockey, flag football, ultimate cutters, rugby sevens, high jump, pole vault, gymnastics, cheer flyers; 50 % beach volleyball, pickleball, padel, rugby backs, goalkeepers, javelin, HYROX; 40 % rugby forwards, cheer bases, throwers, diving, alpine skiing, cycling; 25 % swimming, water polo, rowing, judo, taekwondo, climbing. Off-season cardio 2-4 like your list, 0 extra for swimmers, runners, rowers and endurance cyclists (their practice is the cardio). Your in-season game-heavy rule also for lacrosse, field hockey, rugby, ultimate, flag football and beach volleyball. Recreational players use their activity's share (golf 25 %, running 75 %, rec soccer 60 %); 'a bit of everything' stays 50 %.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT

### R3-ATH-05 — 'Stay athletic and active' (pickleball, HYROX, rec leagues ...): how should play days, cardio and events work?
- **We proposed:** The user can enter the days they play: the hardest gym day stays at least 48 h before the biggest play day, and weeks with 3+ play sessions drop the extra agility drills and half the jumps. A 45-60+ minute session of their activity counts as one of their 3 cardio sessions (up to 2). Optional event date (a first HYROX race, a pickleball tournament, a 10K): the hardest weeks end 1 week before, then 1 easy week at about half the sets.
- **Coach verdict:** ✅ RIGHT
