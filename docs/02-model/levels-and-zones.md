# Levels and zones

This page separates four things that are easy to mix up. The **exercise level** (foundational, intermediate, advanced) says how hard a movement is to do well. It comes from where the load sits, the skill needed and the stability needed, and a seat, machine or cable makes any exercise easier. The **client level** (Level 1, 2, 3) says how much training a person is ready for. The two are separate. The **Davidson zone** is a tag for the position of an exercise and its rotation bias. It is not a level. The **Receive-Create stages** describe how a jump is done. They are not zones. The coach calls levels "still very theoretical", so in this model a level is a soft default and never a gate.

**How to read the tags.** `CAT-04`, `PRG-05` and similar = his round-1 answers (`coach_answers_full.md`). `R2-...` = round 2. `R3-...` = round 3. `R4-...` = round 4 (`coach_answers_round4.md`), the newest questionnaire round. `CT-01` and `CX-01..04` = two clarifications he gave on 2026-10-10 (session time; progression and editing). The newest answer wins on its subject. "chat" = things he said in conversation. **OBSERVED** = read from his sheets. **INFERRED** = our reading. **PROPOSED** = our design, not confirmed by him. "Approved as proposed" = he marked our wording RIGHT without adding words, so the wording is ours and the rule is his.

**Short forms.** DB = dumbbell. KB = kettlebell. SSB = safety squat bar. GHD = glute-ham developer, a bench that holds the feet and hips. BYOP = his "BUILD YOUR OWN PROGRAM" workbook, which holds the MASTER EXERCISE LIBRARY.

His answers on this topic: [../03-coach-answers/levels-and-zones.md](../03-coach-answers/levels-and-zones.md). Where the ideas come from: [../01-philosophy/lineage.md](../01-philosophy/lineage.md).

## 1. Four words, four meanings

| Word | What it describes | Values | Is it a gate? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exercise level** | how hard the movement is to do well | foundational, intermediate, advanced (or no level) | No. A soft default. |
| **Client level** | how much volume the person starts with and can reach | Level 1, Level 2, Level 3 | It sets the dose, not the exercise list. |
| **Davidson zone** | the position of the exercise and its rotation bias | zone 1, zone 2, zone 3 | No. A tag used to pick swaps. |
| **Receive-Create stage** | how a jump is performed | 1 Land, 2 Land & Go, 3 Repeat | No. A progression through the block. |

The word 'ZONE' is written in several places in his sheets with different meanings. Section 6 sorts them out.

## 2. Exercise level: foundational, intermediate, advanced

### 2.1 The rule

**Level = skill + stability + where the load sits.**

| Where the load sits | Level | His example |
|---|---|---|
| in front of you, or low | **foundational** | goblet squat, front-loaded zercher |
| close to and on your body | **intermediate** | barbell front squat |
| behind your body, or overhead | **advanced** | barbell back squat |

**A seat, a machine or a cable makes any position easier.**

**Why (his words, CAT-04).**
- "Mostly, skill and stability demand would be more advanced."
- "The positioning of the bar or the load in front of you would be more foundational because people need to learn how to be more upright."
- "The load closer to and on your body is more intermediate."
- "The loading behind your body, for example, for back squat, would be more advanced."
- "When it's machines or cables, it changes because it's easier because the machine or the cable is doing the work for you."

He added: "In a sense, it's a little bit of a puzzle".

**Round 2 made it precise (R2-LIB-02, approved as proposed).** The level comes from load position, skill and stability, made easier by a seat, machine or cable. It does not come from the zone number. So:
- a deep goblet squat stays foundational;
- a seated dumbbell shoulder press is foundational, even though the weight goes overhead;
- a lat pulldown is foundational, even though the arms are overhead.

### 2.4 Levels add options. They never take options away.

**Rule.** A person who can do an advanced exercise can still be given a foundational one. A level is a default starting point, not a lock.

**Why.** We asked if a higher-level client can still pick lower-level exercises. "Correct, yes. The levels are still very theoretical, so we don't need to rely heavily on them." (CAT-05)

**Safety beats the level label.** His library files leg press, pendulum squat and the hip thrust machine in the MODERATE or ADVANCED blocks, although they are safe and easy to learn. A foundational older or injured client can still get them (EQ-04, approved as proposed). The model offers these machines from the foundational tier for everyone. That wider reading is INFERRED from his sentence about machines doing the work for you (CAT-04). See [equipment.md](equipment.md).

### 2.6 Moving up

**Rule.** A slot can move up a level at the next block, if the exercise felt like '8 - That was perfect' or easier, with no pain (PER-06, approved as proposed).

**Round 3 changed who decides.** There are no check-ins and nothing is logged (R3-VOL-02). So the person or the coach makes that call when building the next block. The plan's guide says so.

### 2.7 How the level is stored

| Field in a catalog record | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `level.primary` | the level of the exercise: from his sheet block ('FOUNDATIONAL', 'MODERATE', 'ADVANCED'), from one of his answers, or INFERRED. `any` = the list has no tiers. `unrated` = no level found anywhere. |
| `level.basis` | which of those three it is |
| `level.byop_placements` | where his sheet files it (kept as history, never overwritten) |
| `level.generator_min_level` | the lowest exercise tier that is offered this exercise by default. Higher tiers can always pick it (CAT-05). |

Counts over the 732 active records (recounted from `synthesis/02_exercise_catalog.json`):

| | foundational | intermediate | advanced | any (no tiers) | unrated |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `level.primary` | 167 | 107 | 122 | 230 | 106 |
| `level.generator_min_level` | 518 | 98 | 116 | | |

Example: Leg Press. `level.primary` = advanced (that is where his sheet files it). `level.generator_min_level` = foundational (safety beats the label). Constraint 5.

**A check we ran (INFERRED).** Load position alone gives the same level as his library for 98 of 176 levelled entries (56 %), and lands within one level for 157 (89 %). The misses are barbell lifts (harder than their position says, because of skill) and machines that he filed higher as volume tools. This check was run in the first version of the equipment model (`synthesis/10_equipment_model.md` §1.4).

**His labels.** The sheet writes 'FOUNDATIONAL', 'MODERATE' and 'ADVANCED' as block labels, and also 'EASY', 'HARD', 'ANY', 'INTERMEDIATE' and 'INTERMIDIATE' in column headers (OBSERVED). The model uses foundational, intermediate, advanced. Spelling is corrected only in what the tool shows, never in his sheets (FIX-04).

## 3. Client level: Level 1, 2, 3

### 3.1 The rule

| Level | Who | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 1** (his 'NOVICE' and 'NEWBY': the same thing) | under about 6 to 12 months of consistent training; **or** back after more than 6 to 12 months off | PRG-05 |
| **Level 2** (intermediate) | consistent training for more than a year | PRG-05 |
| **Level 3** (advanced) | 3 or more years consistent and training 4 or more days a week now, or a competitive physique or strength athlete | **PROPOSED**. He did not define advanced. |
| Pro setting (optional, not a level) | Level 3 only, switched on by the coach: one muscle may go past 30 weekly sets, up to 35 | idea from R2-VOL-06 ("Maybe we can add a pro level"); the numbers are **PROPOSED** |

The model reads the Level 1 / Level 2 line as 12 months of consistent training (INFERRED from PRG-05).

**Why (his words, PRG-05).**
- "a novice is somebody with less than a year of training experience, or maybe less than 6 months around there. A more intermediate athlete can be somebody that has consistently gone to the gym for over a year."
- "If they take off for more than 6 months to a year, then we have to automatically treat them as a newby as far as their body, but not necessarily as their skills, because they might pick up the skills right away."
- "I don't want to make an ex-pro athlete go back to Level 1 for no reason. Maybe just Level 1 for a little bit of time, just so they can get acclimated with it and then be able to move up."
- On the names: "Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 sound okay. That's not a problem. We just have to explain it a little bit better so that they understand it."

So the tool shows the level with its reason in plain words. An example in our wording, not his: "You trained 3 years but took 9 months off, so we start your body at Level 1 for the first block."

### 3.2 What the client level changes

| It changes | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starting weekly sets, big muscles | 10 | 15 | 20 | VOL-01, R2-VOL-04 |
| Starting weekly sets, shoulders, biceps, triceps | 10 | 12 | 15 | R2-VOL-04 (he approved 8 / 12 / 15 and called 8 "probably just barely maintenance"); the floor of 10 lifts Level 1 (R2-VOL-06) |
| Ceiling in a 12-week plan, no Focus muscle | 14 | 22 | 25 | R2-VOL-06 |
| Olympic lifts in athletic training | DB / KB, hex-bar and hang versions | full scope; people early in Level 2 can stay with the Level 1 versions | full scope | ATH-10 ("the beginners and beginning intermediates are going to be more like hang cleans and just these things without bumpers") |
| Strength tests | a 5- to 8-rep max; never a 1-rep max when starting from zero | a 3-rep max, or work up to a 1-rep max, once a block has been completed | same as Level 2 | R2-PER-07 |

The full volume rules, the stairway and the Focus muscles are in [volume.md](volume.md) and [stairway.md](stairway.md). Tests are in [testing.md](testing.md).

### 3.3 What the client level does not change

It does not lock exercises. A Level 1 person who moves well can be given an intermediate or advanced lift. A person back from a long break is Level 1 for volume, but "not necessarily as their skills" (PRG-05).

### 3.4 How the level is worked out

The intake asks three things under "Training experience" (field F17): years of consistent training, months off in the last 2 years, and whether the person is a former competitive or pro athlete.

| Answer | Result |
|---|---|
| under 1 year consistent | Level 1 |
| 1 year or more consistent | Level 2 |
| 3+ years and 4+ days a week now, or competitive physique / strength athlete | Level 3 (PROPOSED) |
| 6 to 12+ months off | body treated as Level 1; the exercise level may stay higher |
| former competitor coming back | Level 1 for a short first stretch, then placed again when the next block is built |

There is no sex field and no question about sleep or stress (GEN-02, R2-GEN-04). Age is asked, and it changes other defaults. See [special-populations.md](special-populations.md).

**Retired.** A fourth level for competition level: competition level changes the athlete profile and the loads, not the volume level (PRG-05, PRG-09). His sheet's ladder with 'NOVICE' below 'NEWBY': they are the same (PRG-05). Moving up a level from logged sessions: nothing is logged (R3-VOL-02).

## 4. Client level and exercise level are separate

**Rule.** The client level is about how much. The exercise level is about how hard the movement is. Do not derive one from the other.

**Why.** We asked if they are separate things. He marked it RIGHT (CAT-06). His own example program shows it (OBSERVED): in the Upper / Lower example a NOVICE does barbell deadlifts and leg press, which the library files as MODERATE and ADVANCED.

The table below gives illustrations of the rule. They are our examples, not coach statements.

| Person | Client level (dose) | Exercise level (default picks) |
|---|---|---|
| New to the gym, moves well | Level 1: starts at 10 sets per group | foundational first; can take harder lifts as skill allows |
| Trained 5 years, took 9 months off | Level 1 body for the first block | can stay intermediate or advanced: skills "pick up ... right away" |
| Consistent 2 years, never used a barbell | Level 2 | foundational on barbell lifts until the positions are learned |
| Older or injured beginner | Level 1 | machines at any filed level; safety beats the label (EQ-04) |
| Advanced bodybuilder | Level 3 | any level; may choose machines to collect volume (EQ-03) |

**One more default that uses the client level.** When an exercise name has no implement ("lunges", "deadlifts", "rows", "calf raises"), the default goes bodyweight, then dumbbell, then barbell as the client's level rises, limited by the equipment they have (EQ-09, approved as proposed).

## 5. Davidson zones

### 5.1 What they are

The coach takes the zones from Dr. Pat Davidson's book *A Coach's Guide to Optimizing Movement* (CAT-04, CAT-10). In his words they are **rotation biases**.

| Zone | Position (his examples) | Rotation bias |
|---|---|---|
| **Zone 1** (vertical, low) | standing, arms down; load low and close to the body | external rotation |
| **Zone 2** (horizontal) | hands in front of you; knee at hip height, halfway through a squat | internal rotation, "more compressive" |
| **Zone 3** (vertical, high) | arms overhead; deep squat | external rotation |

**Rotation bias:** the direction the joints tend to turn in that position. This page keeps to his description and does not add to it.

**His words (CAT-10).** "The zones come from Dr. Pat Davidson's book, A Coach's Guide to Optimizing Movement, which is essentially external rotation biases at the vertical." And: "If I'm in a horizontal plane, like if my knee is at the level of my hips (I'm halfway through a squat), or if my hands are in front of me horizontally, then that is a zone 2 or an internal rotation bias, which is more compressive. It doesn't apply to the plyos. The plyos are different."

### 5.2 How the model uses them

| Rule | Source |
|---|---|
| The zone is a **tag** on the exercise. It does not set the level. | R2-LIB-02 (approved as proposed) |
| Keep **both rotation biases in every week**. | R2-LIB-03 (approved as proposed) |
| Use the bias to **pick a swap when something hurts**. | R2-LIB-03 |
| Do not set it by sport, for now. | R2-LIB-03 |
| Zones do not apply to plyos. | CAT-10 |
| The zone is not used to order or pair exercises in a session. | INFERRED (`synthesis/05_session_architecture.md` §1) |

How the swap is picked (choose an exercise of the same category with the other bias) is our mechanism: PROPOSED.

### 5.3 How the understanding changed

- **Round 1 (CAT-04).** He described zone 1 as the easiest (close to the body, arms low), zone 2 as "very high-pressure, like horizontal, but it's safer for you when you push and pull. That is more intermediate work", and overhead or vertical as more advanced. We first read the zone number as a level step.
- **Round 1 (CAT-10).** He described the zones as rotation biases.
- **Round 2 (R2-LIB-02).** We put both readings to him: zones 1 and 3 are both vertical, external-rotation positions; zone 2 is the horizontal, internal-rotation one; and the exercise level comes from where the load sits, not from the zone. He marked it RIGHT.

So the current rule is: zone = tag, level = load position + skill + stability. `synthesis/10_equipment_model.md` §1.4 was written before round 2 and still describes zones as level steps. The master document and the spec are newer and win.

### 5.4 In the data

- 196 active catalog records carry a zone: 58 in zone 1, 75 in zone 2, 63 in zone 3 (recounted from the catalog). Of these, 174 are resistance exercises, 13 are functional and 9 are core. No athletic record (jumps, throws, Olympic lifts, sprints) and no cardio record carries one.
- The zone number on each exercise is INFERRED from its position. He confirmed the meaning of the zones, not each tag.
- The angle cut-offs between zones are INFERRED. The zone number for a deep squat is INFERRED (he groups the deep squat with the vertical positions).
- No public description of this zone wording was found when the model was built. It is treated as the coach's account of the book. See [../01-philosophy/lineage.md](../01-philosophy/lineage.md).

## 7. Receive-Create stages (not zones)

**Rule.** A jump progression has three stages. Learn to receive force before you create it.

| Stage | Name | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land | receive and pause: stick the landing |
| 2 | Land & Go | receive, then create once: stick, then jump or hop |
| 3 | Repeat | receive and create again and again: hops, repeated jumps |

**Why.** "Your body and your connective tissue need to learn how to receive forces before we teach it how to create." (CAT-10) The name "Receive-Create stages" and the weeks for each stage were approved as proposed (R2-ATH-03).

The stage is a way to perform a jump, chosen by the plan for each part of the block. It is not a fixed property of the exercise. The catalog stores only the stage the name implies. Details, weeks and the plyometric vs ballistic split are in [athletes.md](athletes.md).
