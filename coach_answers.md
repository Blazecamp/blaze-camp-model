## Direct statements made in chat (earlier)
- Different athletes need different things: basketball = most plyos; MMA = many plyos but fewer than basketball; boxing/karate = few plyos. Some populations need more resistance, others more power/athletic patterns.
- Lenses: bigger muscles / smaller muscles / biomechanics; athletic patterns / resistance patterns / core.
- Lineage: Dr. Pat Davidson + Dr. Mike Israetel + first-principles science, NOT packaged models (5/3/1, Texas Method, Prilepin).
- Volume: ~15-20 sets/muscle/week baseline; more for muscles to grow, fewer with pain/injury; weekly ramps ~3/5/7% depending on muscle size; small muscles = more reps (can't go heavy), big muscles = a mix; reps 5-30; preference (lighter/more sets vs powerlifting heavier/fewer) and goal adjust it.
- Week to week: count weekly sets per muscle; to progress, add sets of the same work next week, e.g. chest 20 sets -> 22 sets at the same load, using different exercises for more volume and variety.
- Don't "confuse the muscle": keep the same exercises over a block; change small variables in the next block.
- Ordering: row (horizontal pull) before overhead/vertical pull; horizontal push before vertical push. Power first (athletics) -> power-strength -> strength -> resistance patterns.
- Equipment: machines have many reference points/constraints (sit, feet down, move hands or feet) -> you can push a lot and it's safer -> better for people going up in volume, injured, older. Free weights differ; barbells are more skilled; DB, KB, medballs in between. Clients will state what equipment they have; be ready with references/substitutions.
- Load-velocity: to go faster you don't need much weight; medball upper-body passes/rotations should be light (8-10 lb) so force is produced with speed. Heavier = slower, lighter = faster. Quality of movement matters as much as quantity. Speed, ROM (full/mid), load and volume (sets x reps over time) are all variables.
- The bilateral vs unilateral VOLUME relationship is very important (coach's emphasis).

## Questionnaire answers
- **CAT-01 (tag every exercise on all lenses at once):** "Yes, this is the whole taxonomy of each exercise. This is very important because then we can segment by whatever we need - in different families, different segments, different variables. This is huge."
- **CAT-02 (big = quads, glutes, hamstrings, lats/upper back, chest; small = biceps, triceps, side/rear delts, calves, forearms, abs):** That's right.
- **CAT-03 (is biomechanics its own bucket?):** "No, biomechanics is just the definition of the whole thing; it doesn't have its own bucket of exercises."
- **CAT-04 (what makes FOUNDATIONAL vs INTERMEDIATE vs ADVANCED):** Mostly skill and stability demand, AND load POSITIONING:
  - Load in front of you / front-loaded = foundational (goblet squat, front-loaded zercher) - people need to learn to be upright.
  - Load closer to and on your body = intermediate (barbell front squat).
  - Load behind your body = advanced (barbell back squat).
  - Based on Dr. Pat Davidson's book "A Coach's Guide to Optimizing Movement" ZONES: **Zone 1** = closer to the body at the bottom (e.g. arms low) - easiest; **Zone 2** = horizontal (high-pressure, but safer to push and pull) - intermediate; **Zone 3** = overhead/vertical - most advanced (more positioning, stability and skill needed).
  - Machines or cables change this: easier, because the machine/cable does the stabilizing work.
  - (Implication for us: this likely explains the "Zone 1/2/3" labels on cable curls/tricep extensions, and why the coach orders horizontal before vertical. Treat level = f(skill, stability, load position zone, constraint/implement).)
- **CAT-05 (levels add options, don't remove them):** Correct. "The levels are still very theoretical, so we don't need to rely heavily on them."
- **CAT-06 (client level and exercise level are separate axes):** That's right.
- **CAT-07 (BYOP MASTER EXERCISE LIBRARY = single source of truth):** That's right - "as far as I was able to take it so far."
- **CAT-08 (core thorax vs pelvis rule):** Overall right. The rule: trunk on core (thorax moves), or pelvis on core (pelvis moves), then both at the same time; each can be done in different planes. Toes to bar and Copenhagen planks are more complicated cases.
- **CAT-09 (knee flexion - leg curls, nordics, slider curls):** NOT hip dominant. They belong in a flexion-of-the-leg segment, "kind of like bicep curls" (an isolation-type flexion segment); find the right segmentation for them.
