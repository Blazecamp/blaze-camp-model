## 0. Data facts and counting conventions

### 0.1 There are no dropdowns (data validations) in this tab (OBSERVED)
* `ws.data_validations` is empty for 'BASEBALL', and the sheet XML contains no `<dataValidation>` or `x14:dataValidation` element. **No tab in the MASTER_MODEL workbook has any data validation.** (The BYOP workbook does; its program tabs are dropdown-driven.)
* The '32 data validations' in the brief come from the dump layout. The `DATA VALIDATIONS (dropdown sources):` header in `02_BASEBALL.txt` is empty and is followed directly by **32 HYPERLINKS**. Those 32 entries are YouTube demo links on exercise cells, listed in Appendix A.
* Another 169 exercise cells carry a URL typed into the cell text instead of a hyperlink object. They are counted in Appendix A.
* **Consequence:** BASEBALL is hand-typed free text. Every set, rep and exercise was typed by the coach; nothing was picked from the library. Slot names are typed labels ('bkd', 'horiz pull' ...), not dropdowns.

### 0.2 Date-typed rep cells are reps per side (OBSERVED)
249 rep cells hold a `datetime`. 246 of them have number format `m/d` and 3 have `m-d`. Google Sheets auto-converted typed text such as `10/10` (10 each side) into a date, so the sheet displays **'10/10'**. Every such cell is shown here as displayed: '10/10', '12/12', '8/8', '6/6', '5/5', '4/4', '3/3' for `m/d`, and '8-12' (pull-up rep range) or '10-10' (hyper-extension + KB swing pair) for `m-d`.
The proof is cells that stayed text because the 'month' is impossible: '15/15', '20/20', '12/12/12/12', '10//10'.
The `m/d` cells sit almost only on unilateral, alternating, offset, single-arm or rotational exercises, so they read as **N per side**. A few sit on bilateral implements ('EZ bar Pronated curls' '10/10', 'Z bar bicep curls' '10/10', 'barbell press' '8/8'). There 'N/N' is ambiguous: it could mean N + N, e.g. rest-pause. Rep maths uses N per set, and contact maths doubles only for clearly unilateral athletic rows.
**Bonus (INFERRED, strong):** the stored *year* shows when each cell was typed. All of Block 1 is 2022. Block 2 moves from 2022 copies to 2023 entries. Block 3 is mostly 2023 (table 2.5). Together these date the three blocks.

Testing the tag against the **Part C main lift** of each day across the 22 training weeks (table 7.2): Monday is speed/dynamic or contrast in **15/22** weeks, Tuesday is a strength lift in **19/22**, Thursday is a strength lift in **16/22**, and Friday is speed/dynamic in **19/22**.
So the tags describe the **intent of the main lower-body lift**:
* knee-dominant pattern: speed on Monday (jump squats, KB squat cleans, light 'same speed' squats), strength on Thursday;
* hip-dominant pattern: strength on Tuesday (deadlifts), speed on Friday (KB swings, banded or fast deadlifts, broad jumps).
Each lower pattern therefore gets one speed exposure and one strength exposure per week. This is a dynamic-effort / max-effort split done *inside each pattern*, not across whole days.

**Pairing rules made explicit (OBSERVED in every labelled week):**
1. **Opposite dominance, crossed laterality.** A bilateral *knee*-dominant main lift (bkd) is always paired with a unilateral *hip*-dominant accessory (uhd) on the same day. A bilateral *hip*-dominant lift (bhd) is always paired with a unilateral *knee*-dominant accessory (ukd). Each day therefore covers both knee and hip, and both bilateral and unilateral loading.
2. **Upper pull rides with the knee day, upper push with the hip day:** Mon bkd + horizontal pull, Thu bkd + vertical pull, Tue bhd + horizontal push, Fri bhd + vertical push. Horizontal and vertical alternate within each pair of days.
3. **Upper-body throws go on the pull/knee days (Mon/Thu). Lower-body jumps go in Part A on the push/hip days (Tue/Fri).** Monday's 'Speed' knee-dominant lift is often a jump itself ('DB Squat Jumps', 'Hex bar squat jumps'), and the calf slot often holds ankle hops. So lower-body ballistic work still appears on 3-4 days a week, but only 2 days carry a dedicated lower plyo block.
4. **Isolation follows the compound it complements:** biceps with pull days, triceps and shoulders with push days, calves with the knee-dominant days.
5. **Core rotates through planes:** frontal ('LATERAL CORE', Mon), sagittal ('Sagital core', Thu), transverse ('Transverse core', Tue and Fri). Transverse gets 2 of 4 days, which fits a rotational sport. The BYOP library uses the same plane ladder as its core difficulty levels (sagittal = easy, frontal = moderate, transverse = hard).
6. **Order = most neural first:** ballistic/plyo -> core -> (Olympic) -> heaviest bilateral lift supersetted with an upper compound -> unilateral + small-muscle tri-set -> short sprint 'buy out'.

  * 'shoulders': 'Single Arm DB snatch from floor', 'KB high pull', 'DB HIGH PULL'.
* **Contrast and complex methods appear in pre-season and once in-season:**
  * heavy lift + jump in the same Part C: 'Heavy front squats' 4 x 8 then 'KB squat cleans' 10; 'Barbell deadlift' 4 x 8 then 'Broad jumps' 10 (B2 WK 1); repeated in B3 week 2;
  * light 'same speed' front squats 4 x '4/4/4/4' then 'Jump squats' '8/8/8/8' (B2 'wk 4');
  * 5 rounds of '5 of each' strength-to-plyo complexes (B2 wk 6-WEEK 8).
* **Throws sometimes sit in the core Part:** 'Facing The Wall MB Rotational Scoop' as the 'Transverse core' exercise; 'MEd ball scoop', 'kneeling rotational OH MB slam' and 'MB ROTATIONAL CHEST PASS' as the 2nd core exercise. Rotational power is treated as core training.
* **The buy-out is always a short maximal effort:** 1-3 bouts of 10-20 calories on bike/row/ski or a 15-30 s run, about 3-4 bouts per week in total. It is never long conditioning.
* **The optional Olympic piece is percentage-based and progresses linearly:** 75% x 8 -> 80% x 6 -> (85% x 6) -> 90% x 4, always 4 sets, cleans Mon/Thu and snatches Tue/Fri. It is absent in-season (Block 3), in Block 1 wk 1-5, in Block 2 WK 1-'wk 4', and in the deload.
* **Isolation reps are higher or open-ended:** 'MAX', 'max', 'MAX REPS', 'myo set(s)', 15/15, 20.

### 5.2 Inferred rules (for the generator)
1. **Choose slots first, exercises second.** The template is constant across 24 weeks and 3 seasons. What changes is the exercise (quality: strength / speed / reactive / isometric), the rep scheme and the set count. A generator should store the template as data and vary three dials: *quality per slot*, *reps* and *sets*.
2. **Every slot has a quality dial.** The same 'bkd' slot can be a heavy squat, a tempo squat ('Goblet squat slow', 'SLOW FRONT SQUATS'), a reactive squat ('Oscilating drop squats'), a speed squat ('Front squats - light weights - same speed for all reps') or a loaded jump ('Hex bar squat jumps'). The day tag decides which.
3. **Athletic work is front-loaded and split upper/lower by day,** so no session carries both high-intensity throws and high-intensity jumps in Part A.
4. **Unilateral and frontal-plane work is mandatory** (2 unilateral lower slots per day plus lateral lunges/hops/copenhagens). INFERRED: this reflects baseball's single-leg, rotational and lateral demands.
5. **Shoulder-friendly pressing for a thrower:** every bench press that names an implement is a DB press ('Bottoms up', 'top down', alternating, small incline, flat). The only unnamed one is 'fast banded assisted bench press' (B2 wk 6 and WEEK 8 Tue), and **no row says 'barbell bench'**. Vertical pushing is mostly single-arm / half-kneeling / landmine / seesaw / push press. The only barbell overhead work is 'barbell press' twice in Block 1 and 'barbell overhead strict press' once in Block 2.

## 9. What this reveals about the coach's thinking (INFERRED)

1. **The program is a constant skeleton with dials, not a sequence of different workouts.** The same 6-Part, 4-day template runs unchanged through off-season, pre-season and in-season. Everything the coach 'periodises' is a dial on that skeleton: sets per Part (3/4/5), rep scheme (12/10/8/8 -> 8/6/4/4 -> 4x10), slot quality (strength/speed/reactive/isometric), optional Olympic %, and buy-out dose. A generator can be built the same way.
2. **Categorisation is biomechanical first:** bilateral vs unilateral x knee vs hip dominant for the legs, horizontal vs vertical x push vs pull for the upper body, and planes (frontal/sagittal/transverse) for core. The pairing logic (bkd+uhd, bhd+ukd, pull with knee, push with hip) only makes sense if the buckets are the primary data model. This is the Pat Davidson influence (stance, load position, contralateral/ipsilateral cues, plane-based core).
3. **'Athletic patterns' are a separate track with their own dose metric (time zones and contacts/throws), and they come first.** Upper ballistics on Mon/Thu and lower ballistics on Tue/Fri split the neural load. Ballistic variants also 'leak' into resistance slots (jump squats, swings, lunge jumps, ankle hops in the calf slot) when the day's tag is 'speed'. For a sport profile, the athletic dial = Part A sets x contacts + how many resistance slots are allowed to turn ballistic.
4. **Speed and strength coexist every week inside each movement pattern** (Mon speed / Thu strength for squats, Tue strength / Fri speed for hinges). This is conjugate-like thinking (the workbook has a '(NOT DONE) EXAMPLE CONJUGATE' tab) applied at the pattern level, without the packaged model's max-effort singles.
5. **Volume is set by slots, and hypertrophy volume is a consequence.** About 4 sets x 2 exposures per pattern per week yields about 8-10 sets per pattern and 12-20 per lower-body muscle. Upper-body pushing is deliberately low for a thrower (chest 0-8 sets/week, block means 5-6). The '15-20 sets per muscle' rule is the coach's hypertrophy logic. In this athletic program it is overridden by the sport profile, which is exactly the 'some need more resistance training, some need more power' axis.
6. **Progression is mostly qualitative.** The only % model is the optional Olympic piece. Elsewhere load is cued by words (heavy/light/fast/slow/banded) and progression comes from exercise complexity (bodyweight -> loaded -> unilateral -> contrast), stance changes, and set count +1 for the last 2-3 weeks of a pre-season block (about +3-4%/week), followed by a deload to 3 x 8.
7. **Seasonality drives block choice:** off-season = build (12/10/8/8, broad exercise variety, Oly introduced late). Pre-season = intensify and potentiate (contrast complexes, heavy 4-6 reps, Oly 75 -> 90%, 5 sets, deload before opening day). In-season = maintain (4 x 10, no Oly, fixed sets, short 30-s runs, same plyo/throw exposure, alternating speed/strength days). The four calendars at the top are the planning input for this choice.
8. **Baseball specificity lives in exercise selection, not in the template:** rotational med-ball throws on every upper day ('in-out of hip' scoops, half-kneeling slams, step-back rotational chest passes), copenhagen planks/raises (adductors), staggered-stance deadlifts and box jumps, base-running accelerations ('accelerations baseball starts'), DB-only pressing, and arm care (KB arm bars, half-kneeling shoulder roll). A different sport would keep the skeleton and swap the menus and dials.
9. **Isolation work is AMRAP-centric** (about a third of small-muscle sets are 'MAX'), consistent with 'smaller muscles take more reps' and the legend's inverse VOL columns.
